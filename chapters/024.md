## Parable 4 — When Summer Comes

Again he showed me a great number of trees, but this time they didn't all look alike.

Some were beginning to grow. New life was appearing on their branches, while others remained dry and withered.

“Do you see these trees?” he asked.

“Yes, Sir. Some are sprouting, and some are withered.”

“The trees that are sprouting are the righteous,” he said. “They are the ones who will live in the world to come. For the world to come is summer for the righteous, but winter for sinners.”

I remembered the bare trees he had shown me before, when there had been no visible difference between them. In winter, everything had looked the same. The living stood beside the dead, and I couldn't tell one from another.

But summer was different.

“When the mercy of the Lord shines,” he said, “those who serve God will be revealed. Everyone will be revealed.”

He told me to think about what happens to trees when summer arrives. Their hidden life becomes visible. Leaves unfold, fruit grows, and you can finally see what kind of tree each one is by what appears on its branches.

So it would be with the righteous.

Their fruit would become visible. Even those who seemed insignificant in this world would be seen flourishing in the world to come. What winter had concealed, summer would bring into the open.

But then I looked at the other trees.

They were still withered.

“The Gentiles and the sinners will be like those trees,” he told me. “They will be found dry and without fruit in that world.”

There would be no more uncertainty about what they were. Their lives would be exposed by the fruit they had failed to bear, and the withered trees would be burned like firewood. The sinners would be burned because they had sinned and refused to repent, and the Gentiles because they had not known the One who created them.

Then he turned the parable toward me.

“So bear fruit,” he said, “so that when summer comes, your fruit will be known.”

But bearing fruit required something more than simply wanting to do good. He warned me about filling my life with too much business.

“Stay away from being consumed by too many affairs,” he said. “Those who involve themselves in too much also fall into many sins. Their attention becomes scattered among everything they're trying to manage, and they stop serving their own Lord.”

His question was difficult to avoid.

“How can someone like that ask the Lord for something and expect to receive it when he doesn't serve the Lord?”

Those who serve God can bring their requests to him, he said. But those who do not serve him should not expect to receive from him.

Still, he wasn't telling me that I could do no work at all. A person could give himself to a single occupation and still serve God. His work did not have to corrupt his mind or pull it away from the Lord. He could work while keeping his attention pure.

That seemed to be the danger—not work itself, but becoming so divided among countless concerns that nothing remained undisturbed within me.

The trees stood before me: some coming alive, some still barren.

Winter could hide the difference for a while.

Summer would not.

“If you do these things,” he told me, “you will be able to bear fruit for the world to come.”

And the promise wasn't only for me.

“Everyone who does them will bear fruit.”
