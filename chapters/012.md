## Mandate 4 — Repentance and Purity

“Keep yourself pure,” the shepherd told me. “Don't allow desire for another man's wife to enter your heart, or desire for sexual immorality, or anything like it.”

He was speaking about the heart before he ever spoke about the act.

“If such a desire enters you, you go wrong. If you entertain other desires like it, you sin. For this kind of desire is a serious sin in a servant of God. And when someone carries it into action, he works death for himself.”

Then he gave me the safeguard.

“Remember your own wife, and you will not go wrong.

“Where holiness lives, lawlessness must not be allowed to enter the heart of a righteous person.”

I had questions.

“Sir, may I ask you a few things?”

“Go ahead.”

“If a man has a wife who is faithful in the Lord, but discovers that she's committing adultery, does he sin if he continues living with her?”

“If he doesn't know about it, he doesn't sin,” the shepherd said. “But if he knows and she refuses to repent, continuing in adultery, then by remaining with her he shares responsibility for her sin.”

“Then what should he do if she continues?”

“Let him separate from her and remain alone. If he separates from his wife and marries another, he too commits adultery.”

That raised another question immediately.

“But suppose the wife who was put away repents and wants to return to her husband. Should he take her back?”

“Certainly.”

The answer came without hesitation.

“If he refuses to receive her, he sins and brings serious sin upon himself. A person who has sinned and repented must be received.”

But the shepherd also added a severe boundary.

“Not over and over again. For the servants of God there is one repentance. Because reconciliation remains possible, the husband ought not to remarry.”

He said that the same rule applied whether the offender was husband or wife.

Then he broadened the meaning of adultery beyond sexual misconduct.

“A person commits adultery not only by polluting the body. Whoever lives in the ways of the heathen commits adultery as well. So if someone persists in such deeds and refuses to repent, separate yourself from him. Don't share his life, or you too become a participant in his sin.”

The purpose of remaining unmarried after separation, he said, was that repentance might still lead to reconciliation.

“I'm not saying this to create an excuse for sin,” he told me. “I'm saying it so that the sinner will stop sinning. As for the sin already committed, there is One who can heal it—the One who has authority over everything.”

I still wasn't finished.

“Sir, since the Lord has considered me worthy to have you living with me, please put up with a few more questions. I don't understand much of anything. My heart has become dull because of the way I've lived.”

I couldn't pretend otherwise.

“Help me understand. I'm foolish. I seem unable to grasp anything.”

He answered patiently.

“I oversee repentance. I give understanding to those who repent.”

Then he said something I hadn't considered.

“Don't you understand that repentance itself is a form of understanding?”

I waited for him to explain.

“Repentance is great understanding. A person sins, and then realizes that what he has done before the Lord is evil. The deed enters his heart. He recognizes it, turns away from it, and stops doing evil.”

But repentance wasn't merely stopping.

“He begins doing good abundantly. He humbles himself. His soul grieves over what it has done.”

The shepherd looked at me.

“Do you see now? Repentance is great understanding.”

“That is exactly why I keep questioning you so carefully,” I said. “First, because I'm a sinner. And second, because I don't know what I need to do in order to live. My sins are many, and they aren't all the same.”

“You will live if you keep my commandments and walk in them. Anyone who hears these commandments and keeps them will live to God.”

Still another question had been bothering me.

“I've heard some teachers say there is no repentance other than the repentance we received when we went down into the water and received forgiveness for our former sins.”

“You heard correctly,” he said.

Someone whose sins have been forgiven, he explained, ought to stop sinning and live in purity.

“But because you insist on examining everything carefully, I'll explain this as well, so that no one who already believes in the Lord—or who comes to believe later—can use my words as an excuse for sin.”

He told me that the Lord, who knows every heart and knows beforehand what will come, had seen human weakness. He knew the many ways the devil would attack his servants.

“So the Lord had compassion on what he had made. He established this opportunity for repentance, and authority over this repentance was given to me.”

Then came the boundary.

“If anyone, after this great and holy calling, is tempted and falls into sin, he has one opportunity for repentance. But if someone treats sin casually—sinning, repenting, and then simply sinning again—repentance becomes useless to him. Such a person will find life very difficult.”

Hearing that gave me something I hadn't felt moments earlier.

Hope.

“I feel alive again, sir. Now I know that if I stop adding sins to what I've already done, I can be saved.”

“You will be saved,” he said.

“And so will everyone who does these things.”

I had one final question about marriage.

“If a husband or wife dies and the surviving spouse marries again, does that person sin?”

“No,” he said. “There is no sin in marrying again. But if the person chooses to remain single, he receives greater honor and glory before the Lord. Still, if he marries, he does not sin.”

Then he brought everything back to the center.

“Guard purity and holiness, and you will live to God.

“Everything I'm telling you now, and everything I tell you afterward, guard from this day forward—from the day you were entrusted to me. I will remain in your house.

“Your former sins will be forgiven if you keep my commandments.

“And everyone who keeps these commandments and walks in this purity will receive forgiveness.”
