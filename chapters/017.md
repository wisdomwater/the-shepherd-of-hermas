## Mandate 9 — Ask Without Doubting

“Remove double-mindedness from yourself,” the shepherd said.

Then he described the kind of thought he meant.

“Don't approach God thinking, ‘How can I ask the Lord for anything and expect to receive it after committing so many sins against him?’”

I knew that thought well.

“Don't reason that way. Turn to the Lord with your whole heart. Ask him without wavering, and you will discover how great his compassion is.”

God, the shepherd told me, does not behave like people do.

“He doesn't hold grudges. There is no malice in him. He has compassion on what he has made.”

But I was to clear away the things that crowded my heart.

“Cleanse yourself from the empty concerns of this life and from the things I've already warned you about. Then ask the Lord.”

He spoke with complete confidence.

“You will receive. You will not lack what you ask for, provided you ask without a divided heart.”

The divided heart was the problem.

“If you waver toward God, you won't receive what you're asking for. Those who are double-minded toward him rarely obtain their petitions.”

Faith approached God differently.

“Those who are complete in faith ask while trusting the Lord. They receive because they ask without wavering.”

Then he warned me just how serious double-mindedness could become.

“A person who remains divided in heart and refuses to repent will find salvation difficult.”

So he told me to replace it.

“Cleanse your heart of double-mindedness and clothe yourself with faith, because faith is strong.”

Then he anticipated another difficulty.

What happens when I pray faithfully and nothing seems to happen?

“If you ask the Lord for something and the answer is delayed, don't immediately fall into doubt because you didn't receive what you wanted at once.”

The delay, he said, might be connected to a trial or even some failure in me that I didn't yet recognize.

“But don't stop asking.”

Continue bringing the soul's request before God.

“You will receive it.”

The danger was becoming discouraged and turning the delay into an accusation against God.

“If you grow weary and begin doubting while you pray, blame yourself, not the One who gives.”

Then he told me what double-mindedness really does.

“It is evil and senseless. It uproots people from faith—even people who were once faithful and strong. It is a daughter of the devil and works great evil among God's servants.”

The answer was not to negotiate with it.

“Despise it.

“Master it.

“Clothe yourself with faith, because faith is strong and powerful.”

Faith, he said, expects everything from God and carries its work through.

Double-mindedness does the opposite.

“It has no confidence even in itself, so it fails in everything it tries to do.”

Then he drew the contrast plainly.

“Faith comes from above, from the Lord, and it has great power.

“Double-mindedness is an earthly spirit from the devil. It has no power.”

So the choice was clear.

“Serve the faith that has power.

“Keep away from the double-mindedness that has none.

“Do this, and you will live to God.

“And everyone who lives this way will live to God.”
