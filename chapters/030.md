## Parable 8c — The Many Conditions of the Heart

The shepherd began with the worst of the rods.

“Those whose rods remained withered and eaten through are renegades and traitors to the Church. They sinned, blasphemed the Lord, and became ashamed of the name that had been called over them.”

Not one of them had repented.

“Life has departed from people like these.”

Then he turned to those whose rods had been withered but not eaten.

“These are close to the first group. They were hypocrites who brought in strange teachings and corrupted the servants of God. They especially harmed those who had sinned by refusing to allow them to repent, persuading them instead with foolish teachings.”

But unlike the first group, these still had hope.

Many had already repented after hearing the commandments I had been sent to proclaim. Those who repented became good. Some were placed within the walls, and some even entered the tower.

“Repentance from sin brings life,” he said. “Refusing to repent brings death.”

Then he explained those whose rods had been half withered.

“They are the double-minded. They are neither alive nor dead.”

Those whose half-withered rods also contained cracks were double-minded and divisive. They slandered others, quarreled, and constantly created conflict.

Yet even for these, repentance remained possible.

Some had already repented and entered the tower. Those who repented more slowly remained within the walls. Those who refused to repent would die.

Then he came to those whose rods had been green but cracked.

“These were faithful and good people, but they competed with one another for position and honor.”

He called such rivalry foolish.

When they heard the commandments, most repented quickly and purified themselves, and so they were given a place in the tower. But anyone who returned to division would be cast out.

“The commandments of the Lord say nothing about seeking first place or glory,” he told me. “They speak of patience and humility. Life belongs to those who keep them. Death belongs to those who create factions and act without law.”

Then he explained the rods that were half green and half withered.

“These are people entangled in business who do not remain close to the saints. Half of them lives, and half is dead.”

Many repented after hearing the commandments and entered the tower. Others completely withdrew. Their business dealings had led them so far that they blasphemed and denied the Lord.

They lost their life.

Still others remained uncertain. These could repent if they did so quickly. If they delayed, they would remain outside the tower. If they refused entirely, they too would lose their life.

Next were those whose rods had been two-thirds green and one-third withered.

“These denied the Lord many times.”

Many eventually repented and entered the tower. Others rebelled completely against God and finally lost their life. Some remained double-minded and divisive, and repentance was still available to them if they acted quickly and abandoned the pleasures that had trapped them.

Then came those whose rods had been mostly withered with only a third remaining green.

“These believed at first,” the shepherd said, “but then they became rich and gained honor among the Gentiles.”

Pride grew with their wealth and reputation. They became arrogant, stopped clinging to the righteous, and began living like the Gentiles around them.

They still called themselves believers.

They had not completely left God.

But their lives no longer produced the works of faith.

Many of them repented and received a place in the tower. Others eventually adopted the empty opinions of the Gentiles so completely that they abandoned God and became indistinguishable from them.

Some lost hope because of what they had done. Others became double-minded and created divisions.

For these, too, repentance remained possible—but it needed to come quickly.

Then the shepherd spoke about the rods that had remained almost completely green but had a little withering and cracking at the tips.

“These were always good, faithful, and honorable before God. Their sins were small. They gave in to minor desires and held things against one another.”

When they heard the commandments, most repented immediately and were given a place in the tower.

Some hesitated. Others allowed their hesitation to grow into greater division. But because they had previously lived well, hope of repentance still remained strong for them.

“Very few of these will die.”

Finally he came to those whose rods had originally been almost entirely withered, with only a tiny piece remaining green.

“These believed, but they practiced lawlessness.”

Yet something had remained alive in them.

They never completely separated themselves from God. They continued to bear his name gladly, and they welcomed the servants of God into their homes.

When they heard that repentance was still possible, they seized it without hesitation.

They turned toward righteousness.

Some even willingly endured persecution because they understood what they had done in the past.

“All of these,” he said, “will have their dwelling in the tower.”
