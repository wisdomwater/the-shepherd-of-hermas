## Mandate 7 — Fear the Lord, Not the Devil

“Fear the Lord,” the shepherd said, “and keep his commandments.”

He wasn't talking about fear that left a person helpless.

“If you keep God's commandments, you will become powerful in everything you do, and your work will have unusual strength. When you fear the Lord, you will learn to do things well.”

Then he made a distinction that mattered.

“This is the fear you should have.

“But don't fear the devil.”

That surprised me.

“If you fear the Lord, you will become master over the devil, because there is no true power in him. Where there is no power, there is no reason for fear.”

The Lord was different.

“Where power is glorious, fear is appropriate. The one who possesses power is rightly feared. The one who has none is despised.”

But although I wasn't to fear the devil himself, I was to fear what his works could do.

“Fear the works of the devil because they are evil. If you fear the Lord, you will recognize those works and refuse to do them.”

So fear itself had two sides.

“If you are tempted to do evil, fear the Lord, and that fear will keep you from doing it.

“If you desire to do good, fear the Lord, and that fear will move you to do it.”

This fear, the shepherd told me, was powerful, great, and glorious.

“Fear the Lord, and you will live to him. Everyone who fears him and keeps his commandments will live to God.”

Something in the wording caught my attention.

“Sir, why do you keep saying that those who keep his commandments will live to God?”

“Because every creature fears the Lord,” he said, “but not every creature keeps his commandments.

“The ones who both fear him and keep his commandments have life with God.

“But those who refuse his commandments have no life in themselves.”
