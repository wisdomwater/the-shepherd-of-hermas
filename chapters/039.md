## Parable 9h — Mountains of Life and Death

The seventh mountain had been one of the most beautiful.

Everything upon it flourished. Animals and birds fed on its grass, yet the more they consumed, the more abundantly it grew.

“These are the simple and guileless,” the shepherd said.

They held nothing against one another. They rejoiced in God's servants and were clothed with the Holy Spirit represented by the virgins.

They had compassion on everyone.

From the work of their own hands, they supplied the needs of others without resentment and without questioning whether those people deserved their generosity.

God saw their simplicity and childlike hearts and blessed their work.

“To those who are like this I say: remain this way until the end.”

The eighth mountain was filled with springs from which every creature could drink.

These represented apostles and teachers who carried the word of the Lord throughout the world. They taught with purity and restraint, holding nothing back for selfish desire and walking continually in righteousness and truth.

“They will enter with the angels.”

Then came the ninth mountain.

It had been a wasteland filled with dangerous creatures.

The spotted stones from this mountain represented deacons who had abused their ministry. They had taken what belonged to widows and orphans and used their service for personal gain.

If they continued, they were dead.

But if they returned and served faithfully, they could live.

The mildewed stones represented those who denied the Lord and then isolated themselves from God's people. Like an abandoned vine overgrown with weeds, they became wild and useless.

Repentance was still possible for some, but it needed to come quickly.

The shortened stones represented treacherous people and slanderers.

Their words were like the poisonous animals I had seen on the mountain.

Words could poison.

Words could kill.

Some had already repented and been saved. Others could still do the same.

The tenth mountain was covered with great trees beneath which sheep rested.

“These are bishops and hospitable people.”

They welcomed God's servants into their homes without hypocrisy. They cared for the needy and widows faithfully and carried out their ministry with purity.

“The Lord will shelter those who have sheltered others.”

Their place was with the angels if they remained faithful to the end.

Then he came to the eleventh mountain, whose trees bent beneath beautiful fruit.

“These are those who suffered for the name of the Son of God and willingly gave their lives.”

I remembered that some trees had borne more beautiful fruit than others.

“Why?”

“All who suffered for the Name are glorious before God. But those who were tortured and never denied him, who endured willingly, bear the greater fruit.”

Others had suffered too, but fear had entered their hearts. They had wondered whether they should confess or deny the Lord.

Their fruit was smaller.

Still, they had suffered.

The shepherd warned anyone facing that choice not to nurture thoughts of denial.

Instead, those who suffered for the Name should consider themselves blessed. Their suffering had become a great work, and through it their sins were healed.

“Confess that you have the Lord,” he said. “Put thoughts of denial away from your hearts, so that you may live forever to God.”

Then there was only one mountain left.

The white one.
