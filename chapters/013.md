## Mandate 5 — Sweeter Than Honey

“Be patient and understanding,” the shepherd told me. “Then you will gain mastery over evil and learn to practice righteousness.”

He told me that patience creates room within us.

“When you're patient, the Holy Spirit living within you remains pure. He isn't darkened by another evil spirit. He lives in a wide, open place within you and rejoices there. He serves God cheerfully and lives in peace.”

Anger does the opposite.

“When an angry temper enters, the Holy Spirit, being gentle and delicate, becomes crowded. There is no longer a clear place for him. The evil spirit presses in around him until he has no room to serve the Lord as he wants.”

Then the shepherd put it simply.

“The Lord lives with patience. The devil lives with anger.

“To have both dwelling together in the same person is dangerous.”

He gave me an image.

“Suppose you have a jar of honey and pour a little wormwood into it. Doesn't that small amount make all the honey bitter?”

It did.

“The sweetness is ruined. The owner no longer wants it. A little bitterness has spoiled the whole jar.

“But if you don't add the wormwood, the honey stays sweet and useful.”

That, he said, was the difference between patience and anger.

“Patience is sweeter even than honey. It is useful to the Lord, and he dwells in it. Anger is bitter and useless.”

Even a little anger mixed into patience corrupts it.

“When anger enters, even a person's prayer is no longer useful as it was before.”

I wanted to know how to recognize this angry spirit before it took hold of me.

“Sir, tell me how anger works so that I can guard myself against it.”

“You certainly need to guard yourself,” he said. “You and your household. If you don't, you will lose your hope.”

Then he reassured me.

“But guard yourself, because I am with you. And everyone who has repented with his whole heart must stay away from anger. I will be with them and preserve them.”

Then he explained how anger begins.

“It has little power over people who are full of faith, because the power of the Lord is with them. But it leads empty and double-minded people astray.”

Often, he said, it enters when life is going well.

A person suddenly becomes irritated over worldly matters: food, some trivial inconvenience, a friend, giving something, receiving something—things too small to justify the bitterness they awaken.

“These things are foolish,” he said. “Empty and senseless. They are beneath a servant of God.”

Patience was entirely different.

“Patience is great and strong. It has enormous power. It lives in an open place, rejoicing, free from anxiety, glorifying the Lord at every moment. There is no bitterness in it. It remains gentle and peaceful.”

Then he showed me the progression of anger.

“Anger begins in foolishness. Foolishness gives birth to bitterness. Bitterness gives birth to wrath. Wrath becomes anger, and anger becomes spite.”

What began as something small eventually became something far worse.

“Spite gathers all those evils into itself and becomes a great sin, difficult to heal.”

When all these spirits crowd into the same human vessel in which the Holy Spirit dwells, the vessel can no longer contain them.

“The gentle Spirit isn't accustomed to living with harshness. So he withdraws and seeks a place of gentleness and peace.”

And when the righteous Spirit leaves, the person becomes unstable.

“The evil spirits pull him one direction and then another. He becomes blinded and loses his good judgment.”

That, the shepherd said, was what anger eventually did to a person.

“So stay away from it. It is among the worst of evil spirits.

“Clothe yourself instead with patience. Resist anger and bitterness, and you will find yourself among the holiness the Lord loves.”

Then he gave the command special weight.

“Don't neglect this one, Hermas.

“If you master this commandment, you will be able to keep the others I'm going to give you.

“So become strong in it.

“And let everyone who wants to walk in these commandments become strong as well.”
