# Vision 1 — Play the Man, Hermas

The man who raised me had sold me years earlier to a woman named Rhoda in Rome. Much later, after enough time had passed that our old relationship belonged to another life, I met her again. I came to care for her deeply, but as a sister.

One day I saw her bathing in the Tiber. When she came out, I reached down and gave her my hand, helping her up from the river. She was beautiful, and as I looked at her I had a passing thought: *I would be happy to have a wife like her—someone with her beauty and her character.*

That was all. At least, that was what I told myself.

Some time later I was traveling toward Cumae. As I walked, I found myself caught up in the greatness of creation—the scale of it, the beauty, the sheer power behind everything God had made. Somewhere along the way I fell asleep.

Then something took hold of me.

A Spirit carried me away through a place no ordinary person could have crossed. The land was steep and broken, split open by rushing water, with no clear path through it. Somehow I was brought across a river and into a wide, level country beyond it.

There I fell to my knees.

I began to pray and confess my sins to the Lord. While I was still praying, heaven opened above me, and I saw Rhoda looking down.

“Good morning, Hermas.”

I stared at her. “Lady, what are you doing here?”

“I was taken up,” she said, “so that I could bring your sin before the Lord.”

“My sin?” I said. “Against you?”

“Listen to me. God who lives in heaven, who created everything from nothing and caused it to grow and multiply for the sake of his holy Church, is angry with you because you sinned against me.”

I couldn't believe what I was hearing.

“How did I sin against you? Did I ever say anything indecent to you? Haven't I always honored you? Didn't I treat you like a sister? How can you accuse me of something so shameful?”

She laughed.

“The desire entered your heart.”

That silenced me.

“You think it isn't wrong,” she said, “for an evil desire to enter the heart of a righteous man? It is wrong, Hermas. And it is serious. A righteous person should cultivate righteous purposes. As long as his heart remains fixed on what is right, his life stands secure before God, and he finds the Lord ready to receive him.”

Her voice grew more severe.

“But those who nurture evil intentions bring death and captivity upon themselves. This is especially true of those who give themselves completely to this present world, boasting in wealth and clinging to what they have here instead of longing for the good things still to come. They have abandoned hope, and eventually their souls will grieve over what they chose.”

Then her voice softened.

“Pray to God. He will heal your sins, the sins of your household, and the sins of all the saints.”

The heavens closed.

I was left alone.

Horror settled over me. Grief followed close behind. I kept thinking, *If even that thought is written against me, what hope do I have? How can I possibly be saved? What could I say to God? What words would ever be enough?*

I was turning these questions over and over in my mind when another vision appeared before me.

There was a great white chair, as white as newly washed wool. An elderly woman approached wearing shining clothes, carrying a book in her hands. She sat down alone and greeted me.

“Good morning, Hermas.”

I was still miserable.

“Good morning, lady,” I said, and I began to weep.

She studied me.

“What happened to you? You're normally patient and good-natured. You're always smiling. Why do you look so crushed?”

“An excellent woman told me that I had sinned against her.”

“That kind of behavior is far from a servant of God,” she said. “Still, the thought did enter your heart. And for a servant of God, even such a purpose becomes sin. It is a foolish and dangerous thing for a devout spirit—one already tested—to entertain the desire for evil.”

Then she looked at me almost as though she knew me better than I knew myself.

“And especially you, Hermas. You have lived with restraint. You avoid evil desires. You are simple-hearted, almost guileless.”

I listened, unsure whether I was being comforted or corrected.

“But this isn't the main reason God is angry with you.”

I looked up.

“It is because of your family.”

Her words struck harder than what had come before.

“They have acted wrongly against the Lord and against you, their parents. But because you love your children, you failed to correct them. You allowed things to become terribly corrupted. That is why the Lord is angry with you.”

I had nothing to say.

“Still,” she continued, “the Lord will heal the sins of your past and the sins committed within your household. Their wrongdoing has pulled you into the concerns of this world, but the Lord's mercy has had pity on you and your family. He will strengthen you. He will establish you in his glory.”

Then she leaned into the warning.

“Don't become careless now. Take courage and strengthen your household. Think of a blacksmith striking the same piece of metal again and again until it takes the shape he intends. In the same way, righteous words repeated day after day can overcome evil. Don't stop correcting your children. I know that if they repent with their whole hearts, their names will be written with the saints in the books of life.”

When she finished speaking, she opened the book in her hands.

“Will you listen while I read?”

“Yes, lady.”

“Then pay attention. Listen to the glories of God.”

I listened.

What I heard overwhelmed me. The words were terrible in their power—too great for me to hold onto, almost more than a human being could bear. Most of them disappeared from my memory as soon as I heard them.

But the final words remained.

They were gentler.

“Look,” she read, “at the God of Hosts. By invisible and mighty power, by great wisdom, he created the world. By his glorious purpose he clothed creation in beauty. By his powerful word he fixed the heavens and founded the earth upon the waters. By wisdom and providence he formed his holy Church and blessed her.

“And now he removes heavens, mountains, hills, seas—everything that stands in the way—and makes all things level for his chosen ones, so that he may fulfill the promise he made to them in glory and joy, if they keep with great faith the commands they received from him.”

When she finished, she stood.

Four young men appeared, lifted the white chair, and carried it away toward the east.

Before leaving, the woman called me closer and touched my chest.

“Did you like what I read?”

I hesitated.

“The last part, yes. Those words pleased me. But the earlier words were hard. Difficult.”

“The last words are for the righteous,” she said. “The earlier ones are for the nations and for those who rebel.”

While she was still speaking, two men appeared and took her by the arms. They began leading her away in the same direction the chair had gone, toward the east.

She turned back toward me as she left.

She was smiling.

“Be a man, Hermas.”
