## 1 — The Woman in Heaven

The man who raised me had once sold me to a woman named Rhoda in Rome. Many years passed before I saw her again, and when I did, I loved her as I would a sister. One day I saw her bathing in the Tiber River, and I reached out my hand to help her from the water. As I noticed her beauty, a thought passed quietly through my mind: *I would be fortunate to have a wife with such beauty and goodness.* It was only a thought, and I gave it no further attention.

Some time later, while traveling toward Cumae, I began to marvel at the greatness of God's creation. I walked along praising him for the heavens and the earth, for the strength and beauty with which he had made all things. As I walked, sleep overcame me, and the Spirit carried me away through a wilderness where no person could travel. The land was rugged and broken by waters, but I crossed a river and came at last to level ground. There I fell to my knees and prayed, confessing my sins before the Lord.

While I prayed, heaven opened.

I saw Rhoda above me, greeting me from heaven. Astonished, I asked what she was doing there. She told me that she had been taken up so that my sin might be brought before the Lord.

I did not understand. *What sin have I committed against you?* I asked. I had always treated her with respect. I had loved her as a sister. Why should I be accused because of her?

She reminded me of the thought beside the river.

Then she told me that the righteous must guard even the movements of the heart, for those who intend to serve God should not imagine that the hidden life within them is of no consequence. Yet she did not leave me without hope. She told me to pray, and that God would bring healing to me and to my household.

Then the heavens closed, and I was left alone.

Fear and grief settled over me. If even a passing thought could be brought into the light, how could I stand before God? I began to wonder whether I was lost.

Then I saw before me a great white chair made of wool, and an elderly woman came and sat upon it. She wore shining garments and held a book in her hands. She looked at me and asked why I was sorrowful.

I told her what had happened.

She listened and then corrected me. My trouble was greater than the single thought that frightened me. God had not abandoned me, but my heart had become divided. I had failed to guide my household faithfully, and I had allowed myself to become entangled in the affairs of this life. Yet she spoke not as one announcing my destruction, but as one calling me awake.

She opened the book and began to read words I could not fully understand. Some were severe, and others were glorious.

When she finished, she rose. Four young men came and carried the chair toward the east. Then two others took her by the arms and began leading her away.

Before she disappeared, she turned toward me with a smile.

“Be strong, Hermas.”

And I was left there, still trembling, but no longer certain that trembling was the same thing as being condemned.
