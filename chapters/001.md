# 1 — The Thought of the Heart

The master who raised me had sold me long ago to a woman named Rhoda in Rome. Many years passed before I saw her again, and when I did, I came to love her with the affection of a sister. One day I saw her bathing in the Tiber, and I reached out my hand and helped her from the river.

As I looked at her, I was struck by her beauty and goodness. A thought passed quietly through my heart: *How happy I would be to have a wife like her, beautiful in both appearance and character.* That was all. I did nothing, said nothing, and thought no further of it.

Some time later, while traveling toward Cumae, I found myself marveling at everything God had made—the greatness of creation, its splendor and power. As I walked, sleep overtook me, and a Spirit carried me away through a place no human being could cross. The ground was steep and torn apart by water, broken with deep clefts, but somehow I passed through it and crossed the river beyond.

I came at last to level ground. There I knelt and began to pray to the Lord, confessing my sins.

While I prayed, heaven opened above me.

I saw Rhoda looking down from heaven. “Good morning, Hermas,” she said.

“Lady,” I asked, astonished, “what are you doing here?”

“I was taken up so that I might accuse you of your sins before the Lord.”

I stared at her. “You accuse me?”

“Listen to what I have to tell you. God, who dwells in heaven, who created from nothing all that exists and multiplied it for the sake of his holy Church, is angry with you because you sinned against me.”

“Against you?” I protested. “How? Did I ever speak an improper word to you? Haven’t I always honored you and treated you as a sister? How can you accuse me of such uncleanness?”

She laughed.

“An evil desire entered your heart. Do you think it is nothing for such a desire to enter the heart of a righteous man? It is sin, Hermas, and a serious one. The righteous person sets his heart upon righteous things, and through such purposes he remains steadfast before heaven and finds the Lord merciful in all he does. But those who nurture evil purposes bring death and captivity upon themselves, especially those who cling to this present age, boasting in its riches while giving no thought to the good things that are coming.”

Then she told me to pray. God could heal my sins, the sins of my household, and those of all the saints.

The heavens closed.

Horror seized me. If even that passing thought stood recorded against me, how could I possibly be saved? What words could I use to ask God for mercy?

As these questions churned within me, I saw a great white chair before me, made of wool as white as snow. An aged woman approached wearing shining garments, a book in her hands. She sat alone upon the chair and greeted me.

“Good morning, Hermas.”

Weeping, I returned her greeting.

“Why are you so gloomy? You are usually patient and good-tempered, always smiling. Why are you so downcast?”

I told her what Rhoda had said.

“That deed is far from the servant of God,” she answered. “Yet the thought did enter your heart, and such a thought brings sin to a servant of God. It is a foolish thing for an evil purpose to enter a devout and tested spirit—especially Hermas, who has lived simply and innocently.”

Then her words turned toward something heavier.

“Yet this is not why God is angry with you. Your family has sinned against the Lord and against you, their parents. Because you loved your children, you failed to correct them, and you allowed your household to become terribly corrupted. Through their wrongdoing, you yourself became entangled in the affairs of this world.”

But she did not leave me without hope.

“The Lord in his great mercy has had pity on you and your household. He will strengthen you and establish you in his glory. Do not become careless. Take courage and strengthen your family. As a smith conquers his work by striking it again and again with his hammer, righteous words spoken every day overcome evil. Do not stop correcting your children. If they repent with their whole hearts, they will be written with the saints in the books of life.”

When she had finished, she looked at me and asked, “Will you listen while I read?”

“Yes, lady.”

“Then pay attention,” she said, “and hear the glories of God.”

And I listened.
