## 3 — The Tower Upon the Waters

Blessed are all who practice righteousness. They will never be destroyed.

Among the words entrusted to me was a warning for Maximus: “Tribulation is coming upon you if you choose to deny a second time. The Lord is near to those who turn to him, as it is written of Eldad and Modat, who prophesied to the people in the wilderness.”

Then, brothers and sisters, a revelation came to me while I slept. A young man of extraordinary beauty appeared and asked, “Who do you think the aged woman was who gave you the book?”

“The Sibyl,” I answered.

“You are wrong.”

“Then who is she?”

“The Church.”

I wondered at this. “Why, then, does she appear so old?”

“Because she was created before all things. That is why she is aged. For her sake the world itself was formed.”

Later the aged woman appeared to me in my house and asked whether I had given the book to the elders. When I told her I had not, she said, “You have done well. I still have words to add. When I have finished, everything will be made known through you to all the elect.”

She instructed me to make two copies. One was to go to Clement, who would send it to the cities abroad, for that was his responsibility. The other was to go to Grapte, who would instruct the widows and orphans. I myself was to read the book in the city with the elders who presided over the Church.

After this came my third vision.

I had fasted often and repeatedly begged the Lord to show me the revelation he had promised through the aged woman. That night she appeared and said, “Since you are so persistent and eager to know everything, go into the countryside where you stay. At about the fifth hour I will come and show you what you ought to see.”

I chose a beautiful, secluded place. Yet before I could tell her where it was, she said, “I will come wherever you choose.”

At the appointed hour I went there and found an ivory couch waiting for me. Upon it lay a linen cushion covered with fine flax. No one else was there.

Fear swept through me. I began trembling, my hair standing on end as I realized how completely alone I was. But I remembered the glory of God, gathered my courage, fell to my knees, and once again began confessing my sins.

The aged woman arrived with six young men and listened as I prayed. Then she touched me.

“Hermas, stop continually begging forgiveness for your sins. Pray also for righteousness, so that you may carry some of it back to your family.”

She took my hand and led me toward the couch. To the young men she said, “Go and build.”

When they had gone, she told me to sit.

“Lady, let the elders sit first.”

“Do what I tell you. Sit.”

I tried to sit on her right, but she motioned me toward the left. I was troubled by this, and she noticed.

“Are you sad, Hermas? The place on the right belongs to those who have already pleased God and suffered for the Name.”

“What did they suffer?”

“Beatings, imprisonment, great tribulations, crosses, wild beasts—all for the Name. The right side belongs to them and to everyone who suffers for the Name. The rest sit on the left. Both receive the same gifts and promises, but those on the right possess a particular glory.”

I wanted very much to sit among them, but she told me plainly that I still lacked much. Yet if I remained in simplicity, I would be purified and would one day sit with them, along with all who endured what they had endured.

Then she prepared to leave.

I fell at her feet. “By the Lord, show me the vision you promised!”

She took my hand, raised me, and seated me on the left while she sat on the right. Lifting a shining rod, she asked, “Do you see something great?”

“Lady, I see nothing.”

“Look.”

I looked again.

“Do you not see before you a great tower being built upon the waters from shining square stones?”

Then I saw it.

The six young men were building the tower foursquare. Countless others carried stones toward them, some drawn up from the deep waters and others brought from the land. They placed each stone into the hands of the six young men.

And before my eyes, the tower rose upon the waters.
