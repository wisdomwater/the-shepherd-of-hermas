## Parable 9i — The White Mountain

Those who came from the twelfth mountain were like little children.

“No deceit has entered their hearts,” the shepherd said. “They have not learned wickedness. They remained like infants throughout their lives.”

They had never corrupted God's commandments.

They simply remained innocent.

“Those who remain like children, without deceit, will be more glorious than all those I've described. Children are glorious before God and stand first in his sight.”

He pronounced them blessed.

They had taken off wickedness and clothed themselves in guilelessness.

Then I remembered the bright stones dug from the plain to replace those rejected from the tower.

“What about those stones? And the round stones that were shaped and placed into the tower? And the ones that remained round?”

“The stones from the plain came from the roots of the white mountain.”

The master knew the nature of these people. Because they were innocent, he knew their stones would remain bright once they entered the tower.

None would turn black.

Then the shepherd explained the round stones.

They too came from the white mountain and were naturally bright.

Their problem was their wealth.

Possessions had rounded them, obscuring the truth just enough that they couldn't fit into the tower.

But the Lord saw that their hearts could still favor truth and remain good.

So their possessions were cut down.

Not taken away completely.

Enough remained for them to use in doing good.

That was what I had seen when the shepherd chiseled the bright round stones until they could fit into the building.

The other round stones had not yet received the seal and therefore weren't ready. They had been returned to their place to wait.

The world and the emptiness attached to their possessions still needed to be cut away.

But the shepherd spoke confidently about them.

“The Lord has blessed this innocent kind. Not one of them will perish.”

Even if one were tempted by the devil and stumbled, he would quickly return to the Lord.

Then the angel of repentance spoke directly to everyone who had received the seal.

Remain innocent.

Do not hold grudges.

Do not continue in wickedness or keep replaying bitter offenses in your mind.

Become one spirit.

Heal the cracks between you.

Remove them.

The owner of the flock wanted to find his sheep together and whole.

And the shepherds themselves would be held responsible.

“If the flock is scattered, what will the shepherds say? That the sheep harmed them? No one will believe that. A shepherd cannot blame his flock.”

Then he included himself.

“I am the shepherd, and I above all must give an account for you.”

His urgency increased.

“Change while the tower is still being built.”

There was still time.

The Lord dwells among those who love peace because peace is dear to him. But he keeps himself far from those devoted to conflict and wickedness.

“Return your spirit to him whole, just as you received it.”

Then he gave me another image.

Suppose you handed a cleaner a new garment in perfect condition and later received it back torn.

Wouldn't you be angry?

*I gave this to you whole. Why did you tear it? Now you've made it useless.*

If we cared that much about a damaged piece of clothing, what would the Lord say about the spirit he had entrusted to us whole?

“Do not trample upon his mercy,” the shepherd said. “Glorify him because he has been patient with your sins.”

Repentance was still available.

But it was meant to be used.
