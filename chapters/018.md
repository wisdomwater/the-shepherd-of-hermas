## Mandate 10 — Put Away Sorrow

“Put sorrow away from yourself,” the shepherd told me. “It is the sister of double-mindedness and anger.”

I didn't understand that at all.

“Sir, how can those things be sisters? Anger seems like one thing, doubt another, and sorrow something else entirely.”

“You foolish man,” he said. “Don't you understand that sorrow is more destructive than all these spirits?”

He told me that sorrow could crush the Holy Spirit within a person—and yet, strangely, could also have a part in saving him.

That only confused me more.

“Sir, I don't understand. How can sorrow crush the Spirit and also save?”

“Listen.”

He began with people who had believed but never really pursued the truth. They had become entangled instead in business, riches, relationships with the world, and countless other concerns.

“People who give themselves to these things become unable to understand the things of God. Their minds grow dark. Their lives become barren.”

He compared them to a vineyard.

“A good vineyard can become useless if it's neglected. Thorns and weeds grow everywhere until the vines produce nothing.”

The same thing can happen to a human soul.

“A person believes, then becomes consumed with all these other occupations. Understanding disappears. Even when someone speaks about God and truth, his mind remains buried in his affairs, and he understands nothing.”

But those who fear God and genuinely seek truth are different.

“They direct their hearts toward the Lord. They understand what is said to them more quickly because the fear of the Lord lives within them.

“Where the Lord lives, there is great understanding.

“So cling to the Lord, and you will understand.”

Then he returned to my question.

“Now listen, senseless man, and I'll explain how sorrow crushes the Holy Spirit and how it can also appear to save.”

First came sorrow born from doubt.

“A double-minded person begins some work but fails because he never truly trusted. Then sorrow enters him because he failed.”

That sorrow presses against the Holy Spirit.

Then came sorrow born from anger.

“A person becomes angry over something and grows bitter. Afterward sorrow enters his heart because he sees what his anger caused. He regrets what he did and repents of the evil.”

In that second case, the sorrow appears to lead toward salvation because the person recognizes his sin and repents.

“But both still grieve the Spirit,” the shepherd said.

“Double-mindedness brings sorrow because the person's work failed.

“Anger brings sorrow because the person did evil.

“Both produce grief.”

So he told me not to make a home for sorrow.

“Put it away from yourself. Don't grieve the Holy Spirit who dwells in you, or the Spirit may appeal to God against you and depart.”

The Spirit God had given to live in human flesh, he said, was not made to live under constant sorrow and constraint.

Instead, he told me to put on something else.

“Clothe yourself with cheerfulness.”

Cheerfulness, he said, is pleasing to God.

“Rejoice in it. A cheerful person does good, thinks about what is good, and refuses to surrender himself to sorrow.”

Then he spoke much more severely about the sorrowing person.

“The person ruled by sorrow continually falls into sin. First, he grieves the Holy Spirit, which was given to him as a cheerful spirit. Second, sorrow keeps him from praying and confessing to God as he should.”

He even told me that the prayer of a person overwhelmed by sorrow cannot rise freely to God's altar.

“Why?” I asked. “Why wouldn't his prayer rise?”

“Because sorrow is sitting in his heart.”

Then he gave me another image.

“Think of vinegar mixed into wine. Once they're mixed together, the wine no longer has the same pleasant taste.

“In the same way, when sorrow mixes itself with the Holy Spirit, a person's prayer no longer rises with the same purity.”

So he gave me the command plainly.

“Cleanse yourself from this evil sorrow.

“Clothe yourself with cheerfulness.

“Then you will live to God.

“And everyone who casts sorrow away and clothes himself in cheerfulness will live to God.”
