## Parable 9f — The Stones from the Deep

I turned next to the stones that had risen from the deep.

“Who were the first stones—the ones that entered the tower without needing to be shaped?”

“The first ten are the first generation,” the shepherd said. “The twenty-five are the second generation of righteous people. The thirty-five are God's prophets and ministers. The forty are the apostles and teachers who proclaimed the Son of God.”

“But why did the virgins carry even these through the gate?”

“Because these people bore the spirits represented by the virgins. The spirits never separated from them, and they never separated from the spirits. They remained together until these people fell asleep.”

Without those spirits, even these ancient righteous ones could not have become part of the tower.

But something else bothered me.

“Why did the stones rise from deep water?”

“Because they had to rise through water in order to be made alive.”

Without this, he said, they could not enter the kingdom of God. They had to leave behind the deadness of their former life.

“The seal is the water. They descend into the water dead and come up alive.”

Even those who had fallen asleep before receiving the seal were given it so that they might enter the kingdom.

Before someone bears the name of the Son of God, the shepherd said, he is dead. When he receives the seal, he leaves death behind and receives life.

“But why did the forty stones—the apostles and teachers—rise from the deep with them if they had already received the seal?”

The shepherd explained that after the apostles and teachers had fallen asleep in the faith and power of the Son of God, they proclaimed the Son's name to those who had died before them.

They gave them the seal of the proclamation.

They went down into the water with them.

The apostles went down alive and came up alive.

Those who had died before them went down dead and came up alive.

Through their proclamation, the earlier generations came to know fully the name of the Son of God. That was why they rose together and entered the tower together without needing to be shaped.

They had already fallen asleep in righteousness and purity.

Only the seal had been lacking.

“Now do you understand?”

“I do, Sir.”

Then I returned to the twelve mountains.

“Why are they all so different?”

“The twelve mountains are the twelve tribes inhabiting the whole world. The Son of God was proclaimed among them by the apostles.”

Each mountain represented a different nation, with its own way of thinking and understanding.

But that created another mystery.

“If they're so different, why did their stones all become the same once they entered the tower?”

“Because when the nations heard, believed, and were called by the one name of the Son of God, they received one understanding, one mind, one faith, and one love.”

They carried the spirits of the virgins along with the Name.

That was why the tower became one color, bright as the sun.

Different stones.

One building.

One body.

But some who entered later corrupted themselves and were thrown out, returning to what they had been before—or becoming even worse.

“How could they become worse after knowing God?”

“Someone who does not know God and does evil receives punishment for his wickedness. But someone who knows God should no longer practice evil. He should do good.”

To know and then deliberately turn back, he said, was a greater offense.

That was how the Church would finally be purified.

The wicked, hypocritical, blasphemous, double-minded, and lawless would be removed, just as defective stones had been removed from the tower.

What remained would be one.

One body.

One understanding.

One mind.

One faith.

One love.

“And then,” he said, “the Son of God will rejoice because he has received his people back pure.”
