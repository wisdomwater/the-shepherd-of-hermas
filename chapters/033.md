## Parable 9b — The Tower Above the Gate

Six men appeared, tall, glorious, powerful, and almost identical in appearance.

They called together a great multitude of other strong and handsome men and ordered them to build a tower above the gate in the rock.

Immediately everything became movement.

Men ran back and forth around the gate while the virgins urged them to hurry. The virgins stretched out their hands, waiting to receive whatever would be brought for the building.

Then the six men commanded stones to rise from a deep place.

Ten stones came up first.

They were square and perfectly smooth, though no human hand had shaped them.

The six men told the virgins to carry them through the gate and deliver them to the builders. The virgins gathered around each stone, the strongest taking the corners and the others supporting the sides.

One by one, they carried all ten through the gate.

The builders received them and laid them upon the great rock. Joined together, the ten covered it and became the first foundation of the tower.

Then twenty-five more stones rose from the deep.

The virgins carried these through the gate as well, and they were fitted into the tower.

Thirty-five came next.

Then forty.

Four great rows formed the tower's foundation.

After that, the stones stopped rising from the deep, and the work paused.

The six men ordered stones to be brought instead from the twelve mountains.

These stones looked completely different from one another, carrying the various colors of the mountains from which they came. Men shaped them and brought them toward the tower, but the virgins received them before they entered the building.

Something happened when the virgins carried them through the gate.

Every stone became white.

Whatever color it had possessed before disappeared. Once it passed through the gate in the hands of the virgins, it matched all the other stones.

But some of the men tried handing stones directly to the builders.

Those stones didn't change.

They remained whatever color they had been before, and when they were placed into the tower, they looked ugly beside the others.

The six men immediately ordered them removed.

“Stop handing stones directly to the builders,” they told the men. “Place them beside the tower and let the virgins carry them through the gate. Unless they pass through the gate in their hands, their colors cannot change.”

The men obeyed.

By the end of the day, the tower had risen considerably, though it was not yet complete.

The six men dismissed the builders to rest, but the virgins remained.

They were guarding the tower.

“Sir,” I asked the shepherd, “why wasn't the tower completed?”

“It cannot be completed until its master comes and examines it. If he finds any stones crumbling, they must be replaced. The tower is being built according to his will.”

Naturally, I wanted everything explained immediately.

“What does the tower mean? And the rock? The gate? The mountains? The virgins? What about the stones that came from the deep without being shaped? Why were there ten, then twenty-five, then thirty-five, then forty? And what about the stones that were put into the tower and then removed?”

“If this isn't merely idle curiosity,” he said, “you'll learn everything.”

I had to wait.

A few days later we returned.

“The owner of the tower is coming to inspect it,” the shepherd told me.

When we arrived, no one was there except the twelve virgins.

Then a great procession appeared.

At its center walked a man so tall that he rose above the tower itself. The six glorious men walked beside him, three on either side, followed by the builders and many other glorious attendants.

The virgins ran to him, kissed him, and began walking beside him around the tower.

He inspected everything.

Not generally.

Stone by stone.

He touched every stone with his hands and struck each one with a rod.

Under the blow, their true condition appeared.

Some turned black as soot.

Some became mildewed.

Some cracked.

Some broke short.

Others became neither white nor black.

Some turned rough and no longer fit properly with the stones around them.

Others became covered in spots.

Every unsound stone was ordered out of the tower.

The builders asked which mountain should provide replacements.

“None of them,” the master said.

Instead, he directed them to a nearby plain.

They dug there and found bright stones, some square and some round. Every one was brought to the tower and carried through the gate by the virgins.

The square stones were shaped and fitted into the empty spaces.

The round stones were different. They were bright and beautiful, but so hard and round that shaping them would take considerable work.

So they were placed beside the tower.

They were not rejected.

They simply weren't ready yet.
