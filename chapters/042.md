## Parable 10 — Before the Tower Is Finished

After I had finished writing everything down, the angel who had first entrusted me to the shepherd came to my house. He sat down on the couch, while the shepherd stood beside him on his right. Then he called me over.

“I entrusted you and your household to this shepherd so that he could protect you,” he said.

“Yes, sir,” I answered.

“Then if you want that protection—if you want to be guarded from cruelty and trouble, and to prosper in every good word and good work—walk in the commandments he has given you. If you keep them, you’ll be able to overcome wickedness. Evil desire and the pleasures of this world will lose their power over you, and you’ll find strength for everything good.”

He glanced toward the shepherd. “Learn from his seriousness and his self-control. And tell everyone how greatly the Lord honors him. He has been given enormous authority. In the whole world, he alone has been entrusted with authority over repentance.”

Then his words sharpened.

“Does he seem powerful to you? He is. And yet you people despise the seriousness and restraint with which he treats you.”

I looked at him, then at the shepherd.

“Ask him yourself, sir. Since he came into my house, have I done anything improper? Have I offended him?”

“I already know you haven’t,” the angel said. “And I know you don’t intend to. I’m telling you these things so that you’ll continue as you have begun. The shepherd has given me a good report about you.”

That surprised me more than I expected.

“Now you must tell these things to others. Those who have repented, and those who will repent, should learn to live as you are learning to live. Then he can give me the same report about them, and I can give that report to the Lord.”

“I already tell everyone about the mighty works of the Lord,” I said. “I hope those who have sinned in the past will hear these things, turn back gladly, and recover their life.”

“Then keep doing this work. Carry it all the way to the end. Whoever fulfills these commandments will live and receive great honor from the Lord. But those who refuse them turn away from life itself. They oppose the shepherd, reject his commandments, and hand themselves over to death. They become responsible for their own blood.”

He looked directly at me.

“Obey these commandments. In them you’ll find a remedy for your sins.”

Then he spoke about the virgins.

“I sent them to live with you because I saw that they had become fond of you. You need them. Without their help, you won’t be able to keep these commandments. And I can see that they’re happy to stay with you, so I’ll instruct them not to leave your house.”

There was, however, a condition.

“Keep your house pure. They’re clean, chaste, and diligent, and they have favor with the Lord. They’ll gladly remain wherever they find a clean dwelling. But if even the slightest corruption enters your house, they’ll leave immediately. They cannot live with impurity.”

“I hope I’ll please them, sir,” I said. “I want them to be glad to stay in my house forever. The shepherd has had no complaint against me. I hope they’ll have none either.”

The angel turned to the shepherd.

“I can see that this man wants to live as a servant of God. He’ll keep these commandments, and he’ll give the virgins a clean place to live.”

Then he called the virgins themselves.

“I see that you’re glad to live in this man’s house. So I’m entrusting him and his household to you. Don’t leave his house.”

They received his words gladly.

The angel turned to me one final time.

“Be courageous in this work. Tell everyone about the mighty works of the Lord, and you’ll find favor in this ministry. Whoever walks in these commandments will live and find happiness in life. Whoever neglects them will lose life and find misery instead.”

Then he gave me another charge, and this one reached beyond my own household.

“Tell everyone who has the ability to do good not to stop doing it. People need to be rescued from suffering. Someone who lives every day in need and misfortune is being tormented. Poverty can bind a person as painfully as chains.”

His words grew severe.

“Some people are crushed so badly by these troubles that they can no longer bear them, and they take their own lives. If you know someone is suffering like this, and you have the power to rescue them but refuse, you commit a great sin. You become responsible for their blood.”

There was no room in his words for goodness that remained merely an intention.

“So those of you who have received good things from the Lord—do good while you still can. Don’t delay until the building of the tower is finished. Its construction has been interrupted for your sake. If you don’t hurry to do what is right, the tower will be completed without you, and you’ll find yourselves shut outside.”

When he had finished, he stood up from the couch.

Then he left, taking the shepherd and the virgins with him.

But before he went, he told me they would return to my house.
