## Mandate 2 — Give Without Distinction

“Hold on to simplicity,” the shepherd told me. “Live without deceit. Become like a little child who hasn't yet learned the wickedness that destroys human life.”

Then he began with something very practical.

“Don't speak evil about anyone. And don't enjoy listening when someone else does.”

He told me that listening wasn't as innocent as I might think.

“If you listen to someone slander another person and believe what you're told, you begin carrying the same resentment in your own heart. Then you share responsibility for the sin of the person who spoke.”

He had no patience for slander.

“It is evil. It is like a restless demon that never finds peace and always makes its home wherever people are divided. Stay away from it, and you will be able to live peacefully with everyone.”

Instead, he told me to clothe myself in reverence, where there was no hidden trap and no reason for stumbling.

Then he turned to generosity.

“Do what is good. From the work God gives you and from what you receive through it, give freely to everyone who is in need.”

I expected there to be qualifications.

There weren't.

“Don't keep asking yourself who deserves to receive and who doesn't. Give to everyone, because God desires that his own gifts be shared with everyone.”

“What about the person who receives wrongly?” I wondered.

The shepherd answered that too.

“The person who receives will answer to God for why he received and what he did with it. Someone who accepts help because he is truly in need will not be condemned. But someone who receives through deception will answer for it.”

The burden of judging motives, he told me, did not belong to the giver.

“The giver is innocent. He was given a ministry by the Lord, and if he performs it sincerely, without deciding that one person deserves help while another does not, then he has done what was entrusted to him.”

Such giving, he said, becomes glorious before God.

“The one who serves in this way, sincerely and without discrimination, will live to God.”

Then he brought the commandment back to me and my own household.

“Keep this command as I've given it to you, so that your repentance and your family's repentance may be found genuine.

“Let your heart be pure and undefiled.”
