## Vision 3b — The Stones That Would Not Fit

“Now listen,” the woman said. “I'll explain the stones that are going into the tower.”

I looked again at the shining walls.

“The square white stones, the ones whose edges fit perfectly together, are the apostles, bishops, teachers, and deacons who walked in God's holiness. They served God's chosen people in purity—some of them have already died, while others are still alive.”

She pointed toward the places where the stones met.

“They were at peace with one another. They listened to one another. They lived in agreement. That is why their stones fit together so perfectly in the tower.”

I remembered the stones that had been pulled from deep beneath the water and placed directly into the building.

“And those stones?”

“Those are the ones who suffered for the name of the Lord.”

“What about the stones brought from the land?”

“The ones that go straight into the building without needing to be shaped are those whom the Lord has approved. They walked uprightly before him and faithfully kept his commandments.”

“And the ones that are brought to the tower and placed into it after being examined?”

“They are young in the faith, but they are faithful. The angels warn them to do what is good because wickedness has still been found among them.”

I pointed toward the stones that had been rejected but remained near the tower.

“And those?”

“They have sinned, but they want to repent. That is why they haven't been thrown far away. If they repent, they can still become useful for the building.”

She emphasized the words that followed.

“If they repent now, while the tower is still being built, they can become strong in faith. But once the building is finished, there will no longer be a place for them. Their only advantage now is that they still lie near the tower.”

Then she turned my attention toward the stones that had been smashed and thrown far away.

“Do you want to know about those too?”

“Yes, lady.”

“They are the children of lawlessness. They received the faith hypocritically, while holding on to every kind of wickedness. There is no place for them in the building. They were broken apart and thrown far away because their evil made them useless to the tower and provoked the Lord to anger.”

Around the tower lay still more stones, untouched.

“The mildewed stones,” she continued, “are those who knew the truth but didn't remain in it. They didn't stay close to the saints. So they became useless.”

“And the cracked ones?”

“They are people who carry division against one another in their hearts. Outwardly they appear peaceful, but once they're apart, their resentments remain inside them. Those hidden divisions are the cracks you see in the stones.”

Then she showed me the stones that were too short.

“These have believed, and most of their lives are given to righteousness. But part of them still belongs to lawlessness. That missing part is why the stones are too short. They aren't complete.”

There was one kind I still didn't understand.

“Lady, what about the white round stones? They're beautiful, but they don't fit anywhere.”

She stared at me.

“How long are you going to remain foolish, Hermas? You keep asking about everything and seem to understand nothing.”

I waited.

“These are people who have faith but also possess the riches of this world. When tribulation comes, they deny their Lord because of their wealth and their business affairs.”

“When will they become useful for the tower?”

“When the wealth that leads their souls astray is cut away from them.”

She pointed to one of the round stones.

“A round stone cannot become square unless part of it is cut away. In the same way, people who are rich in this world cannot become useful to the Lord unless some of their riches are cut away.”

Then she turned the lesson toward me.

“You should know this from your own life. When you had riches, you were useless. Now you have become useful and profitable for life. So be useful to God, Hermas. You yourself came from these stones.”

I looked farther out, toward the stones that had rolled away from the tower.

“What about those?”

“The stones thrown far away that roll onto the road and then leave it for places where there is no road are people who believed, but because their hearts are divided, they abandon the true way.”

She gestured toward the barren places beyond the road.

“They think they've discovered a better path. Instead they wander away and become miserable, moving through places where there is no way at all.”

“And the stones that fell into the fire?”

“They are people who finally rebelled against the living God. Repentance no longer enters their hearts because they have surrendered themselves to their cravings and to the wicked things they practice.”

There remained the stones near the water.

They bothered me most.

“And those, lady? The ones that are so close to the water but can't roll into it?”

“They heard the word and wanted to be baptized into the name of the Lord. But when they remembered the purity demanded by the truth, they changed their minds. They turned back and followed their old desires.”

With that, she finished explaining the tower.

But I still wasn't finished asking.

“Lady, is there repentance for all those rejected stones? Is there still some place for them in the tower?”

“They can repent,” she said, “but they cannot be fitted into this tower.”

That wasn't quite the answer I'd hoped for.

“They can be placed somewhere else, somewhere much more humble. But only after they have suffered and completed the days appointed for their sins. They can be changed because they once shared in the Righteous Word.”

She paused.

“If the evil they have done truly comes into their hearts—if they recognize it—then their suffering can come to an end. But if it never reaches their hearts, they will not be saved, because their hearts have become hard.”
