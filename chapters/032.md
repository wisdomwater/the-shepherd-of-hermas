## Parable 9a — The Twelve Mountains

After I had written down the commandments and parables given to me by the shepherd, the angel of repentance, he came to me again.

“I want to show you more fully everything the Holy Spirit showed you before when it spoke to you in the form of the Church. That Spirit is the Son of God.”

He explained that earlier I had been weaker. I wasn't yet strong enough to receive these things through an angel, so the Spirit had appeared to me as the Church, like a virgin instructing me. But I had grown stronger now. I had been strengthened enough to look upon an angel without being overcome by fear.

“So now you must learn these things more accurately from me. This is why the glorious angel appointed me to live with you—so that you can see everything clearly and no longer be terrified as you were before.”

He took me into Arcadia and led me to the top of a rounded mountain.

From there I could see an enormous plain surrounded by twelve mountains.

No two mountains looked alike.

The first was black as soot.

The second was completely bare, without anything growing on it.

The third was covered in thorns and briars.

The fourth had grass growing across it, but the grass was dying. Its upper portions were still green while the roots were withered, and wherever the sun struck fiercely, even more of it dried up.

The fifth was green but rough and rugged.

The sixth was split everywhere by cracks, some small and others enormous. Grass grew inside the cracks, but it was thin and unhealthy.

Then the landscape changed.

The seventh mountain was beautiful and thriving. Its vegetation seemed almost cheerful. Animals and birds fed there, and strangely, the more they ate, the more abundantly the grass grew.

The eighth was covered with springs. Every kind of creature came there to drink.

The ninth was the opposite. There was no water anywhere. The mountain was a wasteland inhabited by wild animals and deadly reptiles that could kill anyone who came near.

The tenth was covered with enormous trees whose branches cast deep shade. Sheep rested beneath them and fed peacefully.

The eleventh was thick with trees bearing every kind of fruit. The fruit looked so good that simply seeing it made me want to eat.

And the twelfth mountain was completely white.

There was something joyful about it.

Of all the mountains, it seemed the most beautiful.

Then I looked toward the center of the plain.

There stood an immense white rock rising higher than any of the mountains. It was square and so enormous that it seemed capable of containing the whole world.

The rock was ancient.

But carved into it was a gate that looked new.

The gate shone more brightly than the sun.

I could hardly look away.

Around it stood twelve virgins. Four stood at the corners and seemed more glorious than the others, though all twelve were glorious. They wore linen tunics, neatly fastened, with their right shoulders left free as though they were preparing to carry something heavy.

They looked eager.

Ready.

I found myself wondering how women who appeared so delicate could stand with such strength, as though they were prepared to carry the heavens themselves.

The shepherd interrupted my thoughts.

“Why are you questioning everything inside yourself and making yourself miserable because you don't understand?”

Apparently, even my silent questions were exhausting him.

“If you're wise, don't struggle with things you cannot comprehend. Ask the Lord for understanding.”

Then he gave me a simple image.

“You can't see what's behind you. You can only see what's in front of you. So leave what you cannot see alone. Master what has been shown to you, and don't torment yourself with curiosity about everything else.”

He promised to explain what I needed to understand.

“For now,” he said, “pay attention to what comes next.”
