## 5 — The Stones That Could Not Fit

I asked about the stones the builders had rejected and thrown aside.

“These have sinned,” the woman told me, “but they desire to repent. That is why they were not thrown far from the tower. If they repent, they can still become useful for the building.”

There was urgency in her words. The tower was still being built, and while the work continued there was opportunity for them to become strong in faith. But once the tower was finished, there would no longer be a place for them. For now, their privilege was simply this: they remained near the tower.

Then she explained the stones that had been broken into pieces and thrown far away. They were children of lawlessness who had received the faith hypocritically while continuing in every kind of wickedness. Their evil made them useless for the building, and the Lord’s wrath had scattered them far from it.

“What about the mildewed stones?” I asked.

“They knew the truth but did not remain in it. They did not cling to the saints, and so they have become useless.”

The cracked stones were those who carried discord within themselves. They appeared peaceful when they were together, but after parting from one another, their resentment remained alive in their hearts. The hidden divisions within them were the cracks I could see in the stones.

The stones that were too short had believed and possessed much righteousness, but some lawlessness remained in them. Something was missing. They were not yet whole.

Then I asked about the white, round stones.

“How long will you remain foolish and stupid?” she replied. “You ask about everything and understand nothing.”

They were people who possessed faith but also the riches of this world. When tribulation came, their wealth and business affairs caused them to deny their Lord.

“When, then, will they become useful for the building?”

“When the wealth that leads their souls astray is cut away. A round stone cannot become square unless something is cut from it. In the same way, those who are rich in this world cannot become useful to the Lord unless their riches are cut away.”

Then she turned the lesson toward me.

“Learn from yourself. When you had riches, you were useless. Now you are useful and profitable for life. You yourself came from among these stones.”

She continued with the stones I had seen rolling away from the tower. These had believed, but because they were double-minded they abandoned the true road. Imagining they could find a better way, they wandered into places where there was no road at all.

Those that fell into the fire were people who had finally rebelled against the living God. Their evil deeds and desires had so consumed them that repentance no longer entered their hearts.

The stones near the water were different. They had heard the word and desired baptism in the name of the Lord. But when they considered the purity demanded by the truth, they changed their minds and returned to their former desires.

With this, she finished explaining the tower.

Still I would not stop asking questions.

“Is there repentance for all these rejected stones? Can they still find a place in this tower?”

“They can repent,” she said, “but they cannot be fitted into this tower. They may be placed somewhere much more humble, but only after they have suffered and fulfilled the days appointed for their sins. Because they participated in the Righteous Word, they may yet be changed. If their evil deeds truly come into their hearts, they will find relief. But if they do not, the hardness of their hearts will keep them from salvation.”

At last I stopped questioning her.

“Would you like to see something else?” she asked.

My joy returned immediately.

She smiled. “Do you see the seven women standing around the tower?”

“I see them, lady.”

“By the Lord’s command, they support it.”

The first had strong hands. Her name was **Faith**, and through her God’s elect are saved. The second was girded for action and looked strong like a man. She was **Continence**, the daughter of Faith. Whoever followed her would turn away from evil desires in the hope of eternal life.

“And the others?”

“They descend one from another. Their names are **Simplicity, Knowledge, Guilelessness, Reverence, and Love**.”

I looked at the seven women surrounding the tower.

“When you do the works of their mother,” she told me, “you can live.”
