## Parable 1 — A City Not Our Own

He spoke to me again, this time about the strange place we find ourselves living.

“You know that you who serve God are living in a foreign land,” he said. “Your own city is far from here.”

Then he asked me a question that made the whole thing feel suddenly practical.

“If you know where your true city is, and you know that one day you will live there, why are you spending so much here? Why are you buying fields, surrounding yourself with expensive things, building houses and rooms you don't need?”

His point was simple. Someone who spends his life establishing himself in a foreign city begins to act as though he has no intention of ever going home.

“You foolish and divided man,” he said. “Don't you understand that everything here belongs to someone else? It's all under another man's authority.”

He told me to imagine living in a city whose ruler could come to me at any moment and say, “I don't want you living here anymore. You don't follow the laws of my city, so leave.”

What would happen then?

I might own fields. I might have houses and possessions, things I had spent years gathering and protecting. But if the ruler ordered me out, none of them could keep me there. Everything I had built would remain behind because it had always belonged to a country that was never truly mine.

And there was an even greater danger.

I already belonged to another city, he said, and that city had its own law. What if I became so attached to my possessions here that I abandoned the law of my true home in order to live comfortably under the law of this one?

Would I trade my citizenship for a field?

Would I give up my home for a house that could be taken from me?

“Be careful,” he warned me. “If you reject the law of your own city, you may find that when you finally want to return, you are no longer received there. You will find yourself shut outside.”

So he told me how to live in this foreign place.

Have enough.

That was all.

Take what is sufficient for the life you have been given here, but don't spend your life fastening yourself to a place you must eventually leave. Live lightly enough that if the ruler of this city tells you to go, you can stand up, leave it behind, and return joyfully to your own city, where you can live according to the law that truly belongs to you.

But he wasn't telling me simply to despise possessions. There was something else I could do with them.

“Remember that you serve God,” he said. “Keep him in your heart. Do his works. Remember his commandments and the promises he has made, and trust that he will fulfill those promises when his commandments are kept.”

Then he told me what kind of property was actually worth acquiring.

Instead of buying fields, use what you have for people who are suffering.

Care for widows. Visit orphans. Don't neglect them. Take the wealth and resources God has placed in your hands and spend them there.

“These are the fields you should buy,” he told me. “These are the houses you should build.”

God had not given people wealth merely so they could surround themselves with more of it. Those who had received much had been given the opportunity to use it in service to him. And unlike the houses and fields of this foreign city, these were possessions they would find waiting for them when they finally reached their own.

He called this kind of spending beautiful.

Joyful, even.

It carried none of the fear that comes from possessing something that can be lost. There was no anxious grasping in it, no need to protect what had been accumulated. It was wealth transformed into mercy, something given away here and somehow found again at home.

“Don't spend the way those around you spend,” he said. “That way of living doesn't belong to the servants of God. Spend according to the way that belongs to you—the kind of spending you can rejoice in.”

And even while living as a stranger, he told me to remain honorable. I wasn't to corrupt what belonged to someone else, take what wasn't mine, or look greedily at another person's possessions.

I had my own work to do.

“Do the work that belongs to you,” he said, “and you will be saved.”
