## Parable 8d — Sent to Call Them Back

When the shepherd had finished explaining all the rods, he turned to me.

“Go and tell everyone to repent, and they will live to God.”

The Lord, he said, had sent him because of his compassion. Repentance had been offered widely, even to some whose actions hardly seemed to deserve another opportunity.

“The Lord is patient,” he said. “He wants those who were called through his Son to be saved.”

After everything I had just seen—the dead rods, the rods revived by water, the cracked ones made whole, and even the nearly lifeless branches producing fruit—I felt hopeful.

“Sir, I believe that when people hear these things, they will repent. Once each person understands what he has done and learns to fear God, surely he will turn back.”

The shepherd's answer was both hopeful and severe.

“Those who repent with their whole heart, cleanse themselves from the evil things I've described, and add no more sins to what they have already done will receive healing from the Lord for their former sins.”

They must not become double-minded about the commandments.

They had to trust them enough to walk in them.

Those who did so would live to God.

But those who returned to their sins and continued chasing the desires of this world would pass judgment upon themselves.

They would choose death.

Then he turned the command directly toward me.

“You walk in my commandments and live to God.”

And again he widened the promise beyond me.

Everyone who walked in them and did what was right would live to God.

The enormous willow had begun as a mystery to me. I had watched its branches cut away without diminishing it. I had watched rods die in people's hands and then, impossibly, seen many of them become green again when planted and watered.

Some had even produced fruit.

The shepherd had shown me how differently people could respond to what they had received. Some held fast. Some cracked under rivalry or division. Some became distracted by wealth and business. Some denied what they had once believed. Some nearly lost everything and yet turned back when repentance was offered.

And some refused.

But wherever repentance was real—where the heart became pure, evil was abandoned, and obedience followed—the dried wood could live again.

When he had finished showing and explaining all these things, the shepherd said one final thing to me.

“After a few days, I will tell you the rest.”
