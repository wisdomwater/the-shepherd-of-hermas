## Parable 8a — The Willow and the Rods

He showed me an enormous willow tree whose branches spread so widely that its shadow covered plains and mountains. Beneath it had gathered all those who were called by the name of the Lord.

Beside the tree stood a glorious angel, towering over everyone around him. In his hand was a great sickle. He cut branches from the willow and handed pieces of them to the people gathered beneath its shade, giving each person a rod about a cubit long.

When everyone had received one, the angel put down his sickle.

I looked back at the willow.

It was completely whole.

I couldn't understand it. I had watched branch after branch being cut away, yet nothing seemed to be missing.

*How can the tree still be whole after so much has been taken from it?*

The shepherd seemed to know what I was thinking.

“Don't be amazed that the tree is still sound. Wait until you've seen everything. Then you'll understand what it means.”

The angel began calling the people back.

One by one, in the same order in which they had received their rods, they returned them to him. He took each rod and examined it carefully.

Some were completely withered and looked as though worms had eaten through them. The angel ordered those people to stand together in one place.

Others returned rods that were withered but not eaten. They were placed in another group.

Some rods were only half withered.

Some were half withered and cracked.

Others were still green but had cracks running through them.

Some were half green and half withered. Others were mostly green with only a third withered, while others were mostly withered with only a third remaining green.

I watched group after group separate from the crowd.

Some rods were almost entirely green but had a little withering at the tip and cracks along them. Others had only a tiny piece of green remaining while everything else had dried up.

Then people began bringing back rods as green as when they had received them.

There were many of these.

The angel was delighted.

Still others returned rods that were green and had begun producing shoots. The angel rejoiced even more over them.

Then came those whose green rods had shoots bearing something like fruit.

The people holding them could hardly contain their happiness.

The angel rejoiced over them, and so did the shepherd beside me.

Then the angel commanded that crowns be brought. They looked as though they had been woven from palm branches.

He crowned those whose rods had produced shoots and fruit and sent them into the tower.

Those whose rods had green shoots but no fruit were also sent into the tower, though instead of crowns the angel placed a seal upon them.

All who entered the tower wore the same clothing, white as snow.

Those who had returned their rods green exactly as they had received them were also sent away with white garments and seals.

When the angel had finished, he turned to the shepherd.

“I am leaving these others with you. Send each one to the place within the walls where he deserves to live. Examine their rods carefully. Don't let anyone escape your attention. And if anyone does escape you, I will test him at the altar.”

Then he left.

The shepherd looked at the rods that remained.

“Let's plant them all and see whether any of them can come back to life.”

I stared at the dried sticks.

“Sir, how can withered rods possibly live?”

“This is willow,” he said. “Willow clings to life. Plant these rods and give them a little moisture, and many of them may live. Then we'll pour water over them as well. If any can be restored, I'll rejoice. And if they can't, at least I won't have neglected them.”

He told me to call the people forward according to their groups.

They came row by row and handed their rods to him. He planted every one of them carefully, arranging them in rows. Then he poured so much water over the ground that the rods disappeared beneath it.

“Now we'll leave,” he said. “After a few days we'll return and inspect them.”

Then he told me why he was willing to go to so much trouble.

“The One who created this tree wants everyone who received a rod from it to live. And I hope that after these rods have received moisture and water, most of them will live.”

But I still couldn't stop thinking about the willow itself.

“Sir, tell me what that tree is. I watched so many branches cut from it, and still it looks untouched.”

“Listen,” he said. “The great tree that overshadows the plains and mountains and the whole earth is the law of God given to the entire world. And this law is the Son of God, proclaimed to the ends of the earth.”

Those standing beneath its shadow were the people who had heard the proclamation and believed.

The great angel was Michael, captain over these people. He was the one who placed the law into the hearts of those who believed, and so he was also the one who examined them to see whether they had kept what they had received.

“The rods are the law,” the shepherd said. “You've seen how many were returned damaged or useless. You will see what becomes of everyone who failed to keep it.”

“Then why did Michael send some people into the tower but leave the others with you?”

“Those who kept the law are under his authority. Those who broke it have been left with me so that they may repent.”

I thought about the people who had been crowned.

“Who were they?”

“They fought against the devil and overcame him. They are those who suffered for the law.”

Those whose rods were green and had shoots but no fruit had been persecuted for the law, but they had neither suffered to the end nor denied it.

And those who returned their rods simply green, exactly as they had received them, were sober and righteous people who had walked with pure hearts and kept the commandments of the Lord.

“As for everyone else,” he said, “you'll understand them after we examine the rods we've planted and watered.”
