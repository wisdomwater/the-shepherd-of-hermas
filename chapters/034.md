## Parable 9c — Stones Given Another Chance

When the inspection was finished, the glorious man who owned the tower called the shepherd to him.

All the stones that had failed his inspection were lying nearby.

“Clean these stones carefully,” he told the shepherd. “Those that can be made to fit with the others, put back into the tower. Those that cannot fit, throw far away.”

Then he left with his attendants.

The virgins remained behind, still guarding the tower.

I looked at the rejected stones.

“How can any of these go back into the tower after they've already failed the inspection?”

The shepherd looked at me.

“Do you see these stones?”

“Yes, Sir.”

“I will shape most of them myself. They will go back into the building, and they will fit with the others.”

That seemed impossible.

“But once you chisel pieces away, they'll be smaller. How can they fill the same spaces?”

“The smaller ones can be placed toward the middle. The larger ones can go toward the outside, where they will hold the others together.”

He was determined to save whatever could still be saved.

We left and returned two days later to begin the work.

First we examined the stones that had turned black.

Nothing had changed.

They remained black, so the shepherd ordered them removed from the tower.

Then he examined the mildewed stones.

Many could still be shaped. He cleaned and cut them, and the virgins carried them into the tower, placing them toward the middle. The others had turned black and were placed with those already rejected.

Then came the cracked stones.

Many could be repaired and shaped. Because they were stronger, the virgins placed them toward the outside of the tower.

But some contained too many cracks.

Nothing could be done with them.

They were rejected.

The shortened stones came next. Many had turned black or developed deep cracks, but some could still be cleaned and reshaped. These were weaker, so the virgins placed them in the middle where stronger stones could support them.

Then we examined the stones that had been half white and half black.

Many were now completely black.

But others had become white.

The virgins carried the white ones into the tower and placed them toward the outside. They were strong enough to help hold the weaker stones together.

We came next to the hard, rough stones.

A few were so hard that they couldn't be shaped at all.

They had to be discarded.

But the rest yielded to the shepherd's work. Once shaped, they too were carried into the tower.

Finally, we examined the spotted stones. A few had turned black, but most became bright and sound. Because of their strength, they were placed toward the outside.

Then the shepherd turned toward the bright, round stones that had been brought from the plain.

“What should we do with these?”

“How should I know, Sir?”

He seemed almost offended by my lack of insight.

“You really don't understand anything about them?”

“Sir, I'm not a mason. I don't know this craft.”

He pointed out the obvious problem. They were extremely round. Making them square would require cutting away a great deal.

Still, some needed to enter the tower.

“Then why worry about it?” I asked. “Choose the ones you want and shape them.”

He selected the largest and brightest. After he cut them down, the virgins carried them into the tower and fitted them along the outside.

The remaining round stones were returned to the plain.

They weren't thrown away.

“There is still a little of the tower left to build,” the shepherd explained. “The master wants these stones in the tower because they are very bright.”

Then twelve other women appeared.

They were beautiful, but unlike the virgins they wore black. Their shoulders were bare, their hair hung loose, and there was something savage about their faces.

The shepherd ordered them to carry away all the stones that had finally been rejected.

They seemed delighted to do it.

They took the stones back to the mountains from which they had originally come.

When nothing remained around the tower, the shepherd and I walked around it together.

It was beautiful.

The stones fit so perfectly that the entire structure looked as though it had been carved from a single piece of rock.

I found myself longing for a building like it.

The shepherd told me to bring plaster and fine clay so we could fill the marks left where stones had been removed and replaced. I brought what he asked for and helped him finish the work.

Then the virgins swept the entire area. They removed every piece of debris and sprinkled the ground with water.

The whole place became clean and beautiful.

“Now,” the shepherd said, “if the master returns, he will find nothing here to blame.”

He started to leave.

I grabbed his bag.

I wasn't letting him disappear before explaining what I had just seen.

He told me he had something to do but promised to return.

“What am I supposed to do here alone?”

“You aren't alone. The virgins are here.”

“Then put me in their care.”

He did.

And left me with them.
