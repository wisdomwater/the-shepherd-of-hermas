## Parable 8b — The Rods That Lived Again

A few days later we returned to the place where the rods had been planted.

The shepherd sat down where the great angel had stood before, and I remained beside him.

“Put on a garment of rough flax,” he told me, “and help me.”

I wrapped myself in a clean but coarse linen garment and stood ready.

“Call everyone whose rod was planted,” he said. “Bring them back in the same order in which they first returned them.”

I went out to the plain and gathered them. They came and stood in their groups.

“Let each person pull up his own rod,” the shepherd said, “and bring it to me.”

The first group came forward—the people whose rods had originally been withered and eaten away.

Nothing had changed.

Their rods were still withered and damaged, and the shepherd ordered them to stand apart.

Then came those whose rods had been withered but not eaten.

Some of these now held green rods.

Others had become even worse, withered and eaten through.

The shepherd separated them according to what had happened.

Then came those whose rods had been half withered or cracked.

Here I began to see astonishing changes.

Many of their rods were now completely green and smooth. Some had become green and produced shoots. Some of those shoots even carried fruit, like the rods belonging to the people who had earlier entered the tower wearing crowns.

Others had not changed at all.

Some remained half withered and cracked. Some had dried completely. Some had become eaten away.

The shepherd placed every person in the proper group.

Next came those whose rods had been green but cracked.

Every one of their rods was now green and whole.

The cracks were gone.

The shepherd rejoiced when he saw them.

Those whose rods had once been half green and half withered came next. Some had become entirely green. Some were still half withered. Others had withered completely and been eaten through, while some had become green and begun producing shoots.

Again, each was placed with the group that matched the condition of the rod.

Then came those whose rods had originally been two-thirds green and one-third withered.

Many had become completely green. Many others remained half withered, while some were entirely withered and eaten.

Those whose rods had been two-thirds withered and only one-third green came after them. Many had improved to half green. Some remained damaged and cracked. Some were still eaten through.

But a few had become completely green.

Then came those whose rods had been almost entirely green except for a little withering and cracking at the ends. Some were now perfectly green. Others had become green and produced shoots.

The last group had once held rods that were almost entirely withered, with only the smallest trace of green remaining.

To my surprise, these had changed more dramatically than almost any others.

Most were now green, producing shoots and even fruit. Others were completely green.

The shepherd was overjoyed.

When he had examined everyone, he turned to me.

“I told you that this tree holds tightly to life. Now do you see how many repented and were saved?”

“I see, Sir.”

“This is so you may understand the greatness of the Lord's compassion. He has shown abundant mercy and given his Spirit to those who were worthy of repentance.”

That immediately raised another question for me.

“Then why didn't everyone repent?”

“To those whose hearts he knew would become pure and who would serve him wholeheartedly, he gave repentance. But he saw that others were crafty and wicked and would only pretend to repent. To them repentance was not given, so that they would not profane his name again.”

I looked across all the groups standing before us, each holding the rod that now revealed what had happened within them.

“Sir, explain who all these people are. Tell me what each kind of rod represents and where each person belongs. Then those who believed, received the seal, broke it, and failed to preserve it may understand what they have done. Perhaps they will repent, receive renewal from you, and glorify the Lord for showing them mercy.”

The shepherd looked at the waiting crowds.

“Listen,” he said.
