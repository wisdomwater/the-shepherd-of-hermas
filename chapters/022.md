## Parable 2 — The Elm and the Vine

I was walking through a field when I noticed two trees growing together, an elm and a vine. I stopped and looked at them for a while, studying the difference between them and the way they seemed to belong together.

The shepherd appeared beside me.

“What are you thinking about?”

“The elm and the vine,” I said. “I was just noticing how well suited they are to each other.”

“These two trees are a picture of the servants of God.”

Of course, that only made me want to know more.

“What do they represent?”

He pointed toward them. “You see the elm and the vine?”

“Yes, Sir.”

“The vine produces fruit,” he said, “but the elm doesn't. Still, if the vine lies along the ground instead of climbing the elm, it can't produce much fruit. Even the little fruit it does produce begins to rot because the vine isn't being held above the ground.”

I looked again.

“But when the vine climbs the elm,” he continued, “it produces good fruit in abundance. So the fruit comes not only from the vine, but from the elm as well.”

That seemed strange to me. The vine was obviously the one producing grapes. The elm was simply standing there supporting it.

“So you see,” he said, “the elm actually produces a great deal of fruit. In a sense, even more than the vine.”

“How can it produce more?”

“Because without the elm, the vine produces very little, and what it produces is spoiled. But supported by the elm, it bears abundantly. This is a parable about the servants of God—the poor and the rich.”

I still didn't see the connection.

“How?”

“Listen,” he said.

A rich person may have an abundance of money and possessions, he explained, yet still be poor in the things of God. His wealth occupies his attention. His prayers and confession before the Lord become small and weak because so much of his life is consumed by what he owns.

The poor person, however, may possess very little and yet be rich in prayer. His intercession before God has great power.

“When the rich person goes to the poor and provides what he needs,” the shepherd said, “he does it believing that what he gives will find its reward with God. So he gives freely, without hesitation.”

And the poor person, receiving what he needs, prays for the one who helped him. He thanks God for the person whose generosity sustained him.

Something begins to happen between them.

The rich person continues giving because he wants the poor person to have what is necessary for life, and because he knows that the poor person's prayers are precious before God. The poor person continues praying, giving back to God the very gift in which he himself is rich.

Neither has everything alone.

The poor person has received the richness of prayer from the Lord, and he offers that richness back through intercession. The rich person has received material abundance from the Lord, and he offers that abundance to the poor. Each gives what has been placed in his hands.

“And this work is great and pleasing to God,” the shepherd said.

The rich person has finally understood what his wealth is for. He no longer treats it simply as something belonging to himself. He recognizes it as something received from God and uses it to carry out God's work by caring for someone in need.

Then the shepherd brought my attention back to the elm.

To anyone passing by, the elm looks fruitless. No grapes hang from its branches. Nothing about it seems productive compared with the vine.

But appearances don't tell the whole story.

When drought comes, the elm still draws water and sustains the vine growing upon it. Because the vine continues receiving what it needs, it continues producing fruit. And so the fruit belongs, in a way, to both of them.

“The poor do the same for the rich,” he told me. “By praying to the Lord for them, they strengthen their riches. And the rich, by providing what the poor need, strengthen their souls.”

Each sustains the other.

Each gives from what God has given.

And together they become partners in the same righteous work.

The shepherd told me that those who live this way will not be abandoned by God. Their names will be written among the living.

Then he spoke one final blessing.

“Blessed are the rich who understand that their riches came from the Lord. Those who understand this will be able to do good with what they have.”
