## 2 — The Little Book

I listened with attention and wonder as the aged woman read. Her words were terrible—so terrible that I could not hold them in my memory. Yet the final words remained with me, perhaps because they were gentler and meant for us.

“Behold the God of Hosts,” she read, “who by invisible and mighty power and great wisdom created the world. He clothed creation with beauty, fixed the heavens by his strong word, founded the earth upon the waters, and in wisdom and providence formed his holy Church and blessed her. He removes heavens and mountains, hills and seas, making everything level for his elect, so that he may fulfill the promise he made to them with great glory and joy—if they keep with great faith the commands they have received.”

When she finished, she rose from the white chair. Four young men came and carried it away toward the East. Then she called me closer, touched my chest, and asked, “Did my reading please you?”

“The last words pleased me, lady,” I answered. “But the earlier ones were difficult and hard.”

“The last words are for the righteous,” she said. “The earlier ones are for the heathen and the rebellious.”

While she was still speaking, two men appeared and took her by the arms. They carried her away in the same direction as the chair, toward the East. She smiled as she departed and called back to me, “Be a man, Hermas.”

A year later, during the same season, I was again traveling toward Cumae. As I walked, I remembered the vision I had received there. Once more the Spirit took me and carried me to that same place.

I fell to my knees and prayed, glorifying the Lord because he had considered me worthy to know my former sins. When I rose, I saw the aged woman again. She was walking before me, reading from a little book.

“Can you report these things to God’s elect?” she asked.

“Lady, I cannot remember so much. Give me the book so I can copy it.”

“Take it,” she said, “but be sure to return it to me.”

I went away to a quiet place and copied the book letter by letter. I could not even make out the syllables, so I faithfully copied each character as I saw it. When I finished, the book was suddenly snatched from my hand, though I could not see who had taken it.

For fifteen days I fasted and earnestly entreated the Lord. Then the meaning of the writing was revealed to me.

My children had sinned against God. They had blasphemed the Lord and betrayed their parents, adding reckless wickedness until their transgressions had reached their full measure. I was commanded to tell them what had been revealed, and also to speak to my wife, who was to be as my sister. She had sinned with her tongue, but when she heard these words she would restrain herself and find mercy.

There was mercy for those who repented with their whole heart and cast double-mindedness from within them. Yet the warning was severe: a limit had been set for the repentance of the righteous. The elders of the Church were therefore to walk carefully in righteousness, so that they might receive the promises of God in their fullness.

“Remain steadfast,” the writing said. “Do not be double-minded, so that you may enter with the holy angels. Blessed are those who patiently endure the great tribulation that is coming and do not deny their life.”

Those who had denied the Lord in former days had received mercy through his great loving-kindness. But those who denied him in the days to come would be rejected from their life.

Then the words turned again to me.

“Hermas, no longer hold a grudge against your children, and do not allow your sister simply to have her own way. Let them be purified from their former sins. They will receive righteous correction, but you must not add your resentment to it, for resentment brings death.”

I had suffered greatly because of my household, but I too had been guilty. I had neglected them while becoming entangled in my own evil affairs.

Yet even there, mercy had not abandoned me.

My salvation was found in this: I had not departed from the living God. Simplicity and self-restraint had preserved me, and if I remained in them, they would continue to preserve me. The same was true for all who walked without deceit and in simplicity.

Such people overcome wickedness.

And they continue into eternal life.
