## 2 — The Book I Could Not Understand

A year passed, and once again I found myself walking toward Cumae. As I traveled, I remembered the vision I had received there—the wilderness, the opening of heaven, the aged woman, and the words that had unsettled my heart. Then the Spirit came upon me again and carried me to the same place.

I fell to my knees and thanked the Lord for remembering me. Even the discovery of my failures had become a kind of mercy, for what is brought into the light can no longer remain hidden. When I rose from prayer, I saw the aged woman walking toward me. She was reading from a small book.

“Can you make these things known to God's people?” she asked.

I looked at the little book and told her I could never remember everything it contained. So she placed it in my hands and told me to copy it.

I withdrew to a quiet place and began writing, letter by letter. Yet the words resisted me. I could copy their shapes, but I could not understand what they meant. When at last I finished, the book was suddenly taken from my hands, though I saw no one take it.

For fifteen days I fasted and prayed.

Then understanding came.

The words spoke first about my own household. My children had wandered far from God, and my wife had wounded others through the careless use of her tongue. I was commanded to speak to them—not to cast them away, but to call them back. If they turned again with their whole hearts, mercy was waiting for them.

And the promise was not only for my family.

Those who had fallen could still rise. Those who had wandered could still return. The past did not have to become the final shape of a life. But repentance could not be merely spoken with the lips while the heart remained divided. We were called to return wholly, putting away the double mind that wanted God while still clinging to everything that drew us away from him.

Later, while I slept, a young man appeared and asked me who I thought the aged woman was.

“The Sibyl,” I answered.

“You are mistaken.”

“Who is she, then?”

“The Church.”

I was bewildered. “Why does she appear so old?”

“Because she was created before all things. For her sake the world was made.”

I carried those words with me, though I did not yet understand them.

Then the aged woman appeared to me again. She asked whether I had delivered her book to the elders. When I told her I had not, she said this was good, because there were still words to be added. When the message was complete, copies were to be sent abroad and read throughout the churches, while I myself was to proclaim it here.

I began to understand that the book had never been given to me merely to satisfy my curiosity.

I had wanted revelation so that I might understand.

But revelation had brought responsibility.

The words I could not read had slowly become words I was required to live—and then to give away.
