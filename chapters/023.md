## Parable 3 — The Trees in Winter

He showed me a great number of trees.

There were no leaves on any of them. Their branches were bare, their limbs exposed, and as I looked across them they all seemed withered. There was nothing I could see that distinguished one tree from another.

“Do you see these trees?” he asked.

“I see them, Sir. They all look the same to me. They look dead.”

“These trees you see are the people who live in this world.”

I looked at them again. If they represented people, I couldn't understand why there was no difference between them.

“Then why do they all look withered?” I asked. “Why do they all look the same?”

“Because in this world, you cannot always tell the righteous from the sinners. They live together, and outwardly they appear alike.”

Then he told me that this world is winter for the righteous.

I thought again about the trees before me. In winter, a living tree can look almost exactly like a dead one. Its leaves are gone. Its branches hold no fruit. Whatever life it carries is hidden where the eye cannot see it.

Stand far enough away, and an entire grove can seem lifeless.

You can look from one bare tree to another and try to decide which will bloom again and which has truly withered, but winter doesn't reveal the answer. For a time, the living and the dead wear the same appearance.

“So it is in this world,” he told me.

The righteous live among sinners, and you cannot always distinguish between them.

For now, they all look alike.
