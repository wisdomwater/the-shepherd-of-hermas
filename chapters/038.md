## Parable 9g — Mountains of Thorns and Cracks

“Now tell me about the mountains themselves,” I said. “I want everyone who hears these things and trusts the Lord to glorify his great and marvelous name.”

The shepherd began with the black mountain.

Those who came from it were rebels, blasphemers, and betrayers of God's servants. Their mountain was black because their ways had become lawless. For these, he said, there was no repentance, but death.

The bare mountain represented hypocrites and teachers of wickedness.

Like their mountain, they produced no fruit.

They carried the name but lacked faith and truth within them. Yet because they had not blasphemed the Lord or betrayed his servants, repentance remained possible if they turned quickly.

The thorn-covered mountain represented the wealthy and those consumed by business.

The briars were riches.

The thorns were endless affairs.

Those consumed with business became so entangled that they drifted away from God's servants. The wealthy sometimes avoided the servants of God because they feared being asked to give something.

“It is difficult for such people to enter the kingdom,” the shepherd said, “just as it is difficult to walk barefoot through briars.”

Still, repentance remained open.

They could make up for the good they had neglected before by beginning to do it now.

The fourth mountain, whose grass was green above but withered at the roots, represented the double-minded.

They spoke of the Lord with their mouths but didn't hold him in their hearts.

Their words lived.

Their works were dead.

When persecution came upon them like the heat of the sun, what little life appeared on the surface dried up. Fear made them ashamed of the Lord's name.

Yet if they repented quickly, they could still live.

The fifth mountain was green but rugged.

These were believers who were faithful but stubborn, slow to learn, and convinced of their own understanding.

They wanted to know everything.

In reality, they understood almost nothing.

Their stubbornness drove wisdom away, yet they praised themselves as wise and tried to make themselves teachers.

“Stubbornness and empty confidence are powerful demons,” the shepherd said.

Some had already humbled themselves, admitted their ignorance, and submitted to people who possessed true understanding.

Repentance remained open to the others because they had been foolish rather than deliberately wicked.

Then he came to the sixth mountain, covered in cracks.

The smaller cracks represented people who spoke against one another. Their words had weakened their faith, but their offenses were still small enough that many repented quickly.

The larger cracks represented something deeper.

These people held grudges.

They continually spoke against one another and kept their anger alive.

Such people were difficult to restore.

The shepherd seemed almost astonished by the absurdity of it.

“God rules all creation, yet when sinners confess, he does not hold a grudge against them. He shows mercy. So why does a mortal human being, himself full of sin, hold a grudge against another person as though he had the power to destroy or save him?”

Then he spoke directly as the angel of repentance.

“Put this away from yourselves. Repent. Cleanse yourselves from this demon, and the Lord will heal your former sins.”
