## Parable 7 — The Affliction of the House

A few days later I saw him again on the same plain where he had shown me the two shepherds.

“What are you looking for?” he asked.

“I came here because I want you to tell the shepherd who punishes to leave my house alone. He's causing me a lot of suffering.”

“You need to be afflicted,” he said. “The glorious angel gave orders concerning you because he wants you to be tested.”

I didn't understand that at all.

“What have I done that's so terrible that I should be handed over to this angel?”

“Listen,” he said. “You have many sins, but not enough to deserve being handed over to him for your own sake. The problem is your household. They have committed serious sins, and the glorious angel was angered by what they had done. That's why he ordered you to suffer for a time—so that they might repent too and cleanse themselves from the desires of this world.”

He told me that when they had truly repented and been cleansed, the angel of punishment would leave.

But that raised another question.

“Sir, if they are the ones who did these things, why am I the one being afflicted?”

“Because there is no other way for the whole household to experience it. You are the head of the house. If you suffer, they suffer with you. But if everything goes well for you, they won't truly feel the affliction.”

I thought about my family.

“But they have repented,” I said. “They've repented with their whole heart.”

“I know they have.”

For a moment I thought that settled the matter.

It didn't.

“Do you think that the sins of people who repent are immediately forgiven?” he asked. “Certainly not.”

He told me that repentance had to reach deeper than regret. The person who repented had to humble himself completely. He had to discipline his own soul and endure the different afflictions that came upon him.

If he endured them, God—the One who created all things and gave them their power—would look upon him with compassion and bring healing.

But there was a condition.

God had to see that the repentant heart had actually been cleansed from evil.

“So it is good for you and your household to endure this affliction now,” he said.

I couldn't argue him out of it.

In fact, he told me to stop trying.

“Why should I keep explaining it to you? You must endure what the angel of the Lord has ordered. He is the one who entrusted you to me.”

Then he told me something I hadn't considered.

I should give thanks.

God had allowed me to know about the suffering before it came fully upon me. I wasn't being left to stumble into it without understanding. I had been warned so that, when it came, I could endure it with courage.

That helped, but not enough.

“Sir,” I said, “if you stay with me, I think I can endure whatever comes.”

“I will stay with you.”

Then, almost gently, he added that he would speak to the angel of punishment and ask him to make the affliction lighter.

“You will still suffer,” he said, “but only for a short time. Afterward you will be restored to your household.”

What mattered now was how I lived through it.

I was to remain humble. I was to serve the Lord with a pure heart, along with my children and everyone in my house. And we were to walk in the commandments he had given me.

“If you do that,” he said, “your repentance will become strong and pure.”

Then he widened the promise beyond my own household.

If we kept the commandments, affliction would withdraw from us.

And not only from us.

“Affliction will stay away from everyone who walks in these commandments.”
