## Mandate 12 — Clothed in Good Desire

“Get rid of every evil desire,” he told me. “Then clothe yourself in the desire that is good and holy.”

He spoke as though desire were something I could put on, something that could cover me and become part of how I moved through the world. If I clothed myself in good desire, he said, I would begin to hate what was evil. I would be able to take hold of those other desires, restrain them, and direct them instead of letting them direct me.

“Evil desire is wild,” he said. “It isn't easily tamed. When someone gets caught in it without understanding what is happening, it can cost him everything. Those who haven't clothed themselves in good desire, who remain tangled up in the life of this world, can be dragged all the way to death by it.”

I wanted to know exactly what he meant.

“What are these evil desires?” I asked. “What do they actually look like? Tell me so I can stay away from them.”

“Listen,” he said.

First, he named the desire for another person's wife or husband. Then the hunger for extravagant wealth, unnecessary delicacies, drink, and every kind of pointless luxury. These things might seem desirable, he said, but for servants of God they were foolish and empty. They belonged to the evil desire, and the evil desire was a daughter of the devil.

“So stay away from them,” he said. “If you let them master you, they will destroy you. But clothe yourself in the desire for righteousness. Arm yourself with the fear of the Lord and resist them.”

Then he gave me another image. When evil desire sees someone standing against it, armed with the fear of God, it becomes afraid. It retreats. And when I had overcome it, I was to bring the prize of my victory to good desire and place myself in her service.

“If you serve good desire,” he told me, “you'll gain power over the evil one.”

That raised another question.

“How do I serve good desire?”

“Practice righteousness and virtue. Practice truth, faith, gentleness, and the fear of the Lord. Do every good thing that belongs with these. Serve good desire in this way, and you will live to God.”

With that, he completed the twelve commandments.

“You have them now,” he said. “Walk in them. Teach those who hear you so that their repentance may remain pure for the rest of their lives. And carry out the work I've given you faithfully to the end. You will accomplish much. Those who are ready to repent will listen to you. I will be with you.”

I had heard all twelve commandments now, and they really were beautiful. There was something in them that could make the heart glad.

But there was also something else I couldn't ignore.

“Sir,” I said, “these commandments are great and beautiful and glorious. Anyone who can keep them would surely rejoice. But I don't know whether a human being actually can. They're very hard.”

His answer came immediately.

“If you decide they can be kept, you will keep them easily, and they won't seem hard. But once you allow yourself to believe that no human being can keep them, you won't keep them at all.”

Then his voice hardened.

“If you neglect them, you will have no salvation—neither you, nor your children, nor your household. You have already passed judgment against yourself by saying that these commandments cannot be kept.”

His anger frightened me.

His whole appearance changed, and I could barely endure looking at him. I stood there shaken and confused until he saw what his words had done to me. Then his manner softened.

“You foolish, confused, double-minded man,” he said. “Don't you understand the greatness of God? He created the world for humanity and placed creation under human authority. If a human being can exercise mastery over the things God has made, do you really think he cannot master these commandments?”

He told me that anyone who truly had the Lord in his heart could keep them. The commandments seemed impossible only to those who carried God on their lips while keeping their hearts hard and distant from him.

“Put the Lord in your heart,” he said, “and you'll discover that these commandments are not hard. They are sweet and gentle.”

Then he turned my fear in another direction.

“Don't be afraid of the devil. He has no power over you. I am the angel of repentance, and I will be with you. I have mastery over him. The devil has fear, but his fear has no strength. Don't fear him, and he will run from you.”

I still wasn't completely satisfied.

“Sir, hear me out. People want to obey God's commandments. Everyone asks the Lord for strength to obey them. But the devil is strong. He overpowers them.”

“He cannot overpower the servants of God who place their whole hope in God,” he answered. “He can wrestle with them, but he cannot throw them down. Resist him, and he will be defeated. He will run away disgraced.”

Then he spoke about jars of wine.

Imagine a man who has filled many jars with good wine, while a few remain empty. When he comes to inspect them, he doesn't worry about the full jars. He knows what is inside them. He examines the empty ones, because emptiness gives room for the wine to turn sour.

“The devil does the same thing,” he said.

He comes to every servant of God and tests them. Those who are full of faith resist him, and he finds nowhere to enter. So he looks for the empty ones. When he finds an opening, he enters and does whatever he wants, until they become his slaves.

“But I am the angel of repentance,” he said again. “I was sent to remain with those who repent with their whole heart and to strengthen them in faith.”

Then his words turned toward those who had already given up on themselves.

If your sins have made you despair of your life, he said, don't add despair to them and make the burden heavier. Turn to the Lord with your whole heart. Practice righteousness for the days that remain. Serve God according to his will, and he will bring healing even to the sins behind you.

As for the devil's threats, he told me not to fear them at all.

“He is as powerless as the tendons of a dead man. Fear God instead—the One who has power to save and to destroy. Keep these commandments, and you will live to God.”

Something in me finally settled.

“Sir, I feel strengthened now because you're with me. I know you will crush the devil's power, and we will overcome his works. And with the Lord helping me, I believe I can keep the commandments you've given me.”

“You will,” he said, “if your heart is pure before the Lord.”

And it wasn't only true for me.

All who purified their hearts from the empty desires of this world could keep them.

All could live to God.
