# 7 — The Renewal of the Spirit

So I fasted for one day. That very night a young man appeared to me.

“You keep asking me for revelations as though they can be given whenever you wish,” he said. “Be careful that all this asking does not injure your body. The revelations you have received are enough. Can you imagine anything mightier than what you have already seen?”

“Sir,” I answered, “I ask only one thing. Tell me the meaning of the three forms in which the aged woman appeared, so that the revelation may be complete.”

“How long will you remain without understanding? Your double-mindedness keeps you from understanding because your heart is not fully set upon the Lord.”

Still I answered, “From you, sir, we will learn these things more clearly.”

“Then listen.”

He began with the first vision, when I had seen the woman as very old, seated upon a chair.

“She appeared aged because your spirit had grown old. It was already decaying and had lost its strength through your weakness and double-mindedness. Think of elderly people who no longer hope to regain their youth and expect nothing except the end of life. You had become like them.”

The affairs of this world had weakened us. We had surrendered ourselves to complaints and anxieties rather than casting our cares upon the Lord. Sorrow had broken our spirits, and beneath its weight we had grown old.

“And why was she sitting upon a chair?” I asked.

“Because a weak person sits so that the chair may support the weakness of the body. That is the meaning of the first vision.”

Then he spoke of the second.

I had seen her standing, her face younger and more joyful, though her flesh and hair remained aged. He told me to imagine an old man who had lost all hope because of weakness and poverty. He expects nothing but the last day of his life.

Then suddenly he learns that an inheritance has been left to him.

At once the man rises. Joy clothes him with strength. The spirit that had been broken by his circumstances is renewed, and instead of remaining seated, he stands with courage.

“So it happened with you when you heard what the Lord revealed. He had compassion on you and renewed your spirits. You put away your weakness, strength returned to you, and you became powerful in faith. The Lord rejoiced when he saw you clothed with strength.”

For this reason, he said, God had shown us the building of the tower. More would be shown if we lived wholeheartedly at peace with one another.

Then he explained the third vision, in which the woman had appeared young, beautiful, and exceedingly joyful.

“When someone who mourns receives good news, he quickly forgets his former sorrow. His attention is taken up by the good news he has received. He is strengthened toward what is good, and joy renews his spirit. In the same way, your spirits were renewed when you saw these good things.”

She had been seated upon a couch because the couch, resting firmly upon four feet, represented stability. Even the world itself, he told me, was upheld by four elements.

“Those who repent completely become young again. They are established firmly because they have repented with their whole heart.”

At last he said, “Now you have the complete revelation. Ask nothing further concerning it. If anything is still lacking, it will be revealed to you.”

Twenty days later, I received my fourth vision, a sign of the tribulation that was coming.

I was walking alone into the countryside along the Companion Way, praying that the Lord would fulfill the visions he had shown me through his holy Church. I asked him to strengthen me and grant repentance to his servants who had stumbled, so that his great and glorious Name might be glorified.

As I gave thanks, I heard what seemed to be a voice.

“Do not be double-minded, Hermas.”

I wondered at this. *How can I be double-minded now? Has not the Lord established me firmly? Have I not seen glorious things?*

I continued walking.

Then, about a stade ahead of me, I saw a cloud of dust rising toward heaven.

*Perhaps cattle are coming,* I thought, *and their feet are raising the dust.*

But the cloud grew larger and larger.

I began to realize that what approached was no ordinary thing.

The sunlight broke through for a moment.
