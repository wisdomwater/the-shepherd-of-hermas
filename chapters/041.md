## Parable 9j — While the Tower Is Still Being Built

At last the shepherd told me that everything he had been sent to say had now been spoken.

“If you believe my words, listen to them, walk in them, and change your ways, you will live. But if you continue in wickedness and keep holding malice against one another, no one who lives that way will live to God.”

Then he looked at me.

“Have you asked all your questions?”

“Yes, Sir.”

“Then why didn't you ask about the marks left in the stones when we placed them back into the tower? Why did we fill those spaces with plaster?”

I had to admit it.

“I forgot.”

Of all the questions I'd asked him, apparently I'd managed to miss one.

“Listen, then.”

Those repaired stones represented people who had heard the commandments and repented with their whole hearts.

The Lord examined their repentance.

It was good.

It was pure.

And he saw that they could continue in it.

So he ordered their former sins to be removed.

That was what the missing pieces of stone represented.

Their old sins had been cut away.

And then even the marks they left behind were filled so that they could no longer be seen.

I thought back over everything the shepherd had shown me.

Twelve mountains had surrounded the plain, each different from the others. Stones had come from every one of them—black places, thorny places, cracked places, fruitful places, places filled with springs, and the beautiful white mountain of innocence.

Yet the stones that passed through the gate in the hands of the virgins had become one color.

One tower.

One body.

I had watched the master inspect that tower stone by stone. Nothing had escaped him. Hidden cracks appeared. Spots emerged. Weaknesses that had been invisible became impossible to conceal.

Yet rejection had not always been the end.

Many stones were handed to the shepherd.

He cleaned them.

He cut away what was damaged.

He reshaped what could still be shaped.

The virgins carried them through the gate again, and they found their place among the others.

Some were strong enough for the outside.

Some needed to be protected in the middle.

But they belonged to the same tower.

That was the work of repentance the shepherd had been showing me all along.

It wasn't pretending the cracks had never existed.

It was allowing what was damaged to be cut away.

It was returning to Faith, Continence, Power, Long-suffering, Simplicity, Guilelessness, Purity, Cheerfulness, Truth, Understanding, Concord, and Love.

It was refusing the clothing of Unbelief, Intemperance, Disobedience, Deceit, Sadness, Wickedness, Wantonness, Irascibility, Falsehood, Folly, Slander, and Hatred.

It was becoming whole again while there was still time.

The tower was not yet finished.

That was the mercy.

The building was still going on.

And while the tower was still being built, the stones could still be restored.
