## Mandate 6 — The Two Angels

“In my first commandment,” the shepherd said, “I told you to guard faith, fear, and self-control.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Now I want you to understand how these powers work.”

He began with two paths.

“Trust righteousness. Don't trust unrighteousness.

“The way of righteousness is straight. The way of unrighteousness is crooked.”

He told me to picture the difference.

“The crooked path has no clear track. It leads through places where there is hardly a road at all. It is rough, filled with obstacles and thorns. Anyone who walks there is harmed by it.

“But the straight way is level. A person can walk it without constantly stumbling. It isn't rough or covered with thorns.

“You can see which path is better.”

“I'd much rather walk the straight one, sir.”

“You will. And everyone who turns to the Lord with his whole heart will walk there.”

Then he told me something else.

“There are two angels with a person: one of righteousness and one of wickedness.”

“How am I supposed to know which one is working in me if both are there?”

“Listen, and learn to recognize them.”

The angel of righteousness, he said, is gentle.

“He is modest, peaceful, and quiet. When he enters your heart, he speaks to you about righteousness, purity, holiness, contentment, every good deed, and every honorable virtue.”

Whenever these things began rising within me, I was to recognize their source.

“When you find these things in your heart, know that the angel of righteousness is with you.

“Trust him. Trust what he produces.”

The angel of wickedness felt very different.

“He is quick-tempered, bitter, and senseless. His works are evil, and they overthrow the servants of God.”

“But how will I recognize him?”

The shepherd began listing the signs.

“When anger or bitterness comes over you, know that he is there.

“When you become consumed with business, with expensive foods and drinking, drunkenness, luxury, lust, greed, pride, boasting, and things like them—when these desires enter your heart, recognize who is speaking.”

Once I recognized his work, there was only one sensible response.

“Keep away from him. Trust him in nothing. His works are evil and useless to the servants of God.”

The difference between the two angels could be seen in what followed their presence.

“Even a faithful person, if he gives himself to the desires of the wicked angel, will eventually sin.

“And even a very wicked person, if the works of the angel of righteousness enter his heart and he follows them, will do something good.”

The shepherd looked at me.

“So you see that it is good to follow the angel of righteousness and say goodbye to the angel of wickedness.”

That, he said, was part of learning faith.

“Trust the works of the angel of righteousness. Do them, and you will live to God.

“Recognize the works of the angel of wickedness for what they are. Refuse them.

“And you will live to God.”
