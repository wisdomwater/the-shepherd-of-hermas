## Mandate 8 — Do Not Restrain the Good

“I told you before that things can be divided in two,” the shepherd said. “Self-control also works in two directions. There are things from which you should restrain yourself, and other things from which you should not restrain yourself at all.”

“Tell me the difference.”

“Restrain yourself from evil and don't do it. But don't restrain yourself from good. Do it freely.”

The distinction was simple.

“If you hold yourself back from doing good, you sin greatly. If you hold yourself back from doing evil, you practice great righteousness.

“So control yourself where wickedness is concerned.

“But do what is good.”

I asked him what kinds of evil he meant.

He began naming them.

“Adultery. Sexual immorality. Drunkenness. Corrupt luxury. Excessive indulgence. Obsession with riches. Pride. Arrogance. Lying. Slander. Hypocrisy. Malice. Blasphemy.”

Then he continued.

“Theft. Deception. Taking what belongs to another. False witness. Greed. Evil desire. Vanity. Boasting, and everything like them.”

These, he said, were deeply destructive things in human life.

“Anyone who serves God must learn to restrain himself from them. A person who refuses such self-control cannot live to God.”

But that was only half the commandment.

“Now listen to the things you must never hold yourself back from doing.”

“Tell me, sir. I want to understand the power of the good too, so that I can walk in it.”

“Then listen.”

First came the qualities at the center of a righteous life.

“Faith. Fear of the Lord. Love. Harmony. Righteous speech. Truth. Patience.

“There is nothing better than these in human life. Anyone who holds on to them and never restrains himself from practicing them will be blessed.”

Then he moved from qualities to actions.

“Care for widows.

“Visit orphans and those in need.

“Free the servants of God from their afflictions when you can.

“Practice hospitality, because hospitality creates opportunities for kindness.

“Don't retaliate against anyone.

“Live quietly.

“Consider yourself beneath others rather than above them.

“Honor the elderly.

“Practice righteousness.

“Preserve fellowship with your brothers and sisters.

“Endure injury.

“Be patient.

“Don't hold grudges.”

The list kept going.

“Encourage people whose souls are weak.

“When someone has stumbled away from the faith, don't throw that person aside. Turn him back and give him courage.

“Correct those who sin.

“Don't oppress people who owe you money or those who are poor.

“And do everything else that resembles these things.”

Then he asked me, “Do these seem good to you?”

“Sir, what could be better?”

“Then walk in them. Don't hold yourself back from doing them, and you will live to God.”

The commandment, in the end, worked in both directions.

“Do good, and never restrain yourself from it.

“Refuse evil, and restrain yourself from it.

“Everyone who keeps these commands and walks in them will live to God.”
