## Mandate 11 — Test the Prophet by His Life

The shepherd showed me a group of men sitting together on a couch. Nearby, another man sat alone on a chair.

“Do you see the men on the couch?” he asked.

“Yes, sir.”

“They are faithful. But the man sitting on the chair is a false prophet. He destroys the minds of God's servants—not those who are strong in faith, but those who are divided within themselves.”

He told me that these doubtful people went to the man the way others went to a fortune-teller.

“They ask him what is going to happen to them. But there is no divine Spirit in him. He simply answers according to their questions and according to what they want to hear.”

The false prophet was empty, the shepherd said, and so were those who came to him.

“An empty man gives empty answers to empty people. Whatever they ask, he tells them something that fits the emptiness already inside them.”

Still, the deception wasn't always obvious.

“Sometimes he says things that are true. The devil can fill him with his own spirit in the hope that he might even bring down someone righteous.”

But those who were firmly rooted in faith did not cling to such people.

“They are clothed in truth, so they keep their distance. It is the doubtful-minded, those who are constantly changing direction, who run after soothsaying like the Gentiles do.”

The shepherd's judgment was severe.

“A person who consults a false prophet becomes an idolater. He has emptied himself of truth and acted without understanding.”

There was a fundamental difference between the Spirit of God and the spirit these people sought out.

“No Spirit that comes from God needs to be consulted like a fortune-teller. The divine Spirit has power and speaks from above. It speaks when God wills.”

The other kind was very different.

“The spirit that waits for people to question it and then tells them what they want is earthly, unstable, and powerless. It doesn't speak unless someone comes asking.”

That raised an obvious question.

“Then how can anyone tell the difference? How do we know who is a true prophet and who is false?”

“Listen carefully,” he said. “You will test both of them by the way they live.”

He began with the person who truly carries the Spirit from above.

“He is gentle. Peaceful. Humble. He keeps away from wickedness and from the empty desires of this world. He considers himself beneath everyone else.”

The true prophet was not eager to perform.

“He doesn't give answers whenever someone comes demanding them. He doesn't withdraw somewhere private and start speaking simply because someone wants a prophecy.”

The Holy Spirit, the shepherd said, was not something a person could summon on command.

“The man speaks when God wants him to speak.”

Then he described how that happened.

“When someone who carries the divine Spirit enters a gathering of righteous people—people who themselves have faith in the Spirit—and they pray together to God, then the angel of the prophetic spirit fills that person.”

Only then does he speak.

“Filled with the Holy Spirit, he speaks to the gathered people according to what the Lord wills.”

“That,” the shepherd said, “is how the Spirit of God reveals himself.”

Then his attention shifted to the other kind.

“Now listen to the life of the earthly and empty spirit.”

The contrast was immediate.

“The man who only appears to have the Spirit exalts himself. He wants the most important place. He becomes bold, shameless, and talkative.”

He surrounds himself with luxury and deception.

“And he takes money for his prophecies. If no one pays him, he doesn't prophesy.”

The shepherd almost seemed offended by the idea.

“Can the divine Spirit be bought? Can a prophet of God take money in exchange for speaking?”

The answer was obvious.

“No. The spirit in such a prophet is earthly.”

There was another sign.

“He avoids gatherings of righteous people. He stays away from them.”

Instead, he seeks out people who are as empty and uncertain as he is.

“He meets them privately and tells them what they want to hear.”

The shepherd gave me an image.

“Put an empty vessel beside another empty vessel. Neither one breaks. They fit together perfectly because they are both empty.”

But bring that same false prophet into a gathering filled with righteous people, and everything changes.

“When righteous people who possess the Spirit of God gather and pray, the false prophet is emptied. The earthly spirit inside him flees in fear.”

The man who seemed so confident before suddenly has nothing to say.

“He becomes silent. He is completely undone because no word comes to him.”

The shepherd gave me another comparison.

“Imagine a storeroom packed with containers of wine and oil, with one empty jar placed among them. Later, when you unpack the room, what will you find in that jar?”

“Nothing.”

“Exactly. It went in empty and came out empty.”

That was what happened to false prophets when they entered the presence of people filled with the Spirit of God.

“They are revealed exactly as they truly are.”

Then the shepherd summed it up.

“I have shown you the life of both kinds of prophets. So if someone claims to be moved by the Spirit, test him by his life and by what he does.”

Don't trust the claim itself.

“Trust the Spirit that comes from God and carries power. Put no trust in the earthly and empty spirit. It has no power because it comes from the devil.”

Then he gave me one final parable.

“Pick up a stone and throw it toward heaven. See whether you can reach it.

“Or take some water and spray it upward. See whether you can pierce the heavens.”

I looked at him.

“Sir, how could anyone do either of those things? They're impossible.”

“Exactly.”

He smiled.

“Earthly spirits are like that. They have no real power.”

Then he told me to look in the opposite direction—from heaven toward earth.

“Think of hail. A piece of hail can be tiny, but when it falls from above and strikes someone's head, you know how much it can hurt.”

Or a drop of water.

“A single drop falling again and again from a roof can wear its way into stone.”

He let the image settle.

“Do you see? Even the smallest thing that comes from above can carry great power when it reaches the earth.

“The divine Spirit is the same. It comes from above, and it is powerful.

“Trust that Spirit.

“Stay away from the other.”
