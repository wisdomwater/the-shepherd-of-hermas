## Parable 9d — A Night with the Virgins

The virgins welcomed me warmly.

The four who had seemed most glorious were especially kind toward me.

As the day passed, they told me the shepherd would not return until later.

“Then I'll wait until evening,” I said. “If he hasn't come by then, I'll go home and return tomorrow morning.”

“You can't leave.”

“Why not?”

“You were entrusted to us.”

I wasn't sure what they expected me to do.

“Then where am I supposed to stay?”

“With us. You'll spend the night here as our brother, not as a husband. You are our brother, and from now on we will live with you. We love you dearly.”

I felt embarrassed at the thought of staying among them.

Then the woman who seemed to lead the others came over and embraced me and kissed me. The others followed her, greeting me with the same affection.

They led me around the tower.

Something changed in me as I walked with them.

I felt young again.

Some of the virgins danced. Some skipped. Others sang.

Before long, I found myself sharing in their joy as we walked around the tower together.

When evening came, I tried again to leave.

They wouldn't allow it.

So I stayed.

They spread their linen garments on the ground beside the tower and made a place for me to sleep among them. Nothing happened through the night except prayer.

They prayed constantly.

So did I.

Their joy seemed to increase when they saw that I prayed as earnestly as they did.

I remained with them until morning.

Around the second hour, the shepherd finally returned.

He looked at the virgins.

“Did you do him any harm?”

“Ask him,” they said.

I answered for myself.

“Sir, I was glad to stay with them.”

“What did you eat for supper?”

“The words of the Lord. All night.”

“Did they treat you well?”

“Yes, Sir.”

“Good. Now what do you want to hear first?”

I had been waiting for that question.

“Everything in the order you showed it to me. Let me ask from the beginning, and explain each thing exactly as we come to it.”

“As you wish,” he said. “I'll explain everything to you. I won't hide anything.”

So I began with the things that had first caught my attention.

“The rock and the gate. What are they?”

“The rock and the gate are the Son of God.”

That only created another question.

“How can the rock be ancient while the gate looks new?”

“Listen and understand, foolish man.”

The Son of God, he told me, existed before all creation and shared in the Father's work of creation. That was why the rock was ancient.

The gate was new because the Son had been revealed in the last days.

“Those who are to be saved enter through this gate into the kingdom of God.”

He reminded me of the stones.

Those carried through the gate became part of the tower. Those that did not pass through it were rejected.

“No one enters the kingdom of God unless he receives the name of his Son.”

He compared it to a walled city with only one entrance.

“If a city has one gate, can you enter any other way?”

“Of course not.”

“So no one enters the kingdom of God except through the name of his beloved Son.”

The great multitude building the tower were glorious angels surrounding the Lord.

The gate was the Son.

There was no other entrance.

Then he reminded me of the towering man who had come to inspect the building.

“That glorious man is the Son of God. The six who walk beside him are the glorious angels who guard him.”

Even these angels, he said, did not enter into God apart from the Son.

“Whoever does not receive his name will not enter the kingdom of God.”
