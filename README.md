# The Shepherd of Hermas

## A Contemplative Modern Retelling

This repository contains a modern contemplative retelling of *The Shepherd of Hermas*, an influential early Christian work traditionally dated to the second century.

The goal of this project is not to produce a new scholarly translation or commentary. It is to make the experience of reading *The Shepherd* more immediate by retelling it in natural twenty-first-century English while remaining faithful to the imagery, teachings, conversations, and sometimes unsettling strangeness of the original.

Hermas should sound like a person rather than an ancient manuscript.

He sees visions he doesn't understand. He asks questions. He misunderstands what he is shown. His heavenly guides correct him, encourage him, and occasionally become impatient with him. Slowly, he learns.

The retelling tries to preserve that humanity.

## Approach

The text follows the natural structure of the original work:

* **Visions**
* **Mandates**
* **Similitudes / Parables**

Rather than modernizing the source sentence by sentence, each section is retold as a flowing narrative. Dialogue is preserved wherever possible, imagery is allowed room to breathe, and moments of fear, wonder, repentance, confusion, and hope are gently expanded when the source supports them.

The theology, however, is not modernized.

*The Shepherd of Hermas* comes from a Christian world nearly two thousand years removed from our own. Some of its teachings about repentance, obedience, judgment, purity, and transformation may feel unfamiliar or even uncomfortable to modern readers. This retelling intentionally allows those tensions to remain.

The purpose is to hear an ancient Christian voice through modern language, not to make that voice agree with contemporary Christianity.

## Reading Hermas Today

At the heart of *The Shepherd* is a concern that remains remarkably contemporary:

**What are we becoming?**

Hermas presents the Christian life as a process of transformation. Stones are shaped so they can become part of a tower. People fail and are given opportunities to repent. Wealth creates responsibility toward those who suffer. Desire must be confronted. Truthfulness, generosity, faithfulness, and self-control must become more than ideals.

Readers do not need to agree with every conclusion Hermas reaches in order to listen to him.

Part of the value of reading an ancient work is allowing it to remain genuinely ancient—letting it challenge our assumptions without requiring us to replace them wholesale with its own.

## About This Retelling

This project was created with AI assistance through an iterative process of reading, retelling, reviewing, and refining the source text.

Each retelling was produced from specifically supplied sections of *The Shepherd of Hermas*, with strict boundaries intended to prevent material from later passages or other editions from being introduced into an entry.

The guiding principles were simple:

Preserve the story.

Preserve the strangeness.

Preserve Hermas's questions.

Let the language become modern without making the theology modern.

The result is intended as a contemplative literary retelling rather than a replacement for a scholarly translation. Readers interested in textual history, translation differences, or academic study should consult critical editions and established translations of the original work.

## A Final Thought

Hermas lived in a world very different from ours, yet the question running through his visions remains recognizable.

How do imperfect people actually become different?

His answers are sometimes beautiful, sometimes severe, and sometimes strange. They come from another time and another Christian imagination, but they are worth hearing.

We can listen without needing to agree with everything we hear.

And perhaps, after walking beside Hermas for a while, we may find ourselves carrying his question with us:

**What am I becoming?**

The tower is still being built.
